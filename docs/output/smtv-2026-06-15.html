$BTC added +1% to $66,280, $ETH jumped +4.5% to $1,737, and $SOL ran +2.3% to $72. Total crypto market cap sat at $2.25 trillion, parked on the exact level that held as support back in early February. The whole complex is coiled in the same four-month range it has been stuck in all spring.[6:35]

The detail worth flagging is correlation. When the peace headline hit the wire overnight, equity futures popped and Bitcoin popped right alongside them. That is a change. For most of the spring crypto sat out the equity tape entirely, drifting in its own range while stocks made new highs. Seeing BTC trade as a clean risk-on asset again, in the same direction as the Dow on the same catalyst, is the kind of behavior that tends to show up before crypto tries to leave a range.[7:01]

The macro backdrop is about as friendly as it gets for a BTC breakout. The dollar is soft, crude collapsed more than five percent, metals caught a strong bid, and stocks are at record highs with a calm bond tape underneath. On paper that mix should pull crypto higher. The screen is still only showing a modest move, which says positioning is quiet and the next real leg needs a fresh catalyst that the range has not yet been handed.

The Dow Jones Industrial Average just closed its highest week in recorded history and added another full percent Monday morning, a 500-point move to 52,100. The trigger was an overnight headline claiming a ceasefire in the Middle East. The desk's own read on that headline was open skepticism, with Spencer saying he would take the under on the peace actually holding. The tape did not care. Crude got smoked more than five percent toward $80, metals ripped, and the NASDAQ tacked on two percent back above 30,000.[5:48]

The bigger structural story was Friday's SpaceX IPO. It went off without a single hitch. The company issued roughly 550 million shares and nearly all of them traded on the first day, which is the kind of clean absorption that almost never happens on a deal that size. The desk framed it as proof that the traditional IPO machine still works even for a company this large and this strange, and called it possibly the most successful debut in market history. The forward read matters more than the day itself. With the SpaceX print landing cleanly, the expectation now is that OpenAI and Anthropic move up their own timelines and price in July or August, with both likely trading by Labor Day.[12:39]

Under the surface the rally stayed broad, which is the part that keeps the desk constructive. Small-cap financials, meaning the regional and community banks, broke out of an eight-year base to their highest weekly close ever. Midcap industrials, the machinery and engineering names that sit at the core of the US economy, are pushing higher. Small caps are making new highs relative to the mega-caps, which undercuts the tired story that only a handful of stocks are holding the market up. Even Italy and Spain closed at all-time highs. A tape where the regional banks and the airlines lead is not the tape that usually marks a top.[17:14]

Chapter 3 · The Framework

How to Spot a Cherry-Picked Scare Chart, Using the One Going Viral Now

The bearish chart making the rounds this week came from Bloomberg. It claims that the first three months under a new Fed chair have historically delivered an average 12 percent drawdown. The US just installed a new Fed chair on May 22, so the implication is that a big drop is overdue. JC spent a full segment calling it fake news, and the way he took it apart is a useful lesson for anyone who gets rattled by a scary stat with an official-looking source.[13:08]

The trick is in the average. That 12 percent number is dragged down almost entirely by two Fed chairs, Eugene Meyer and Eugene Black, both installed in the depths of the Great Depression right after the 1929 crash. Of course stocks fell hard after those two took over, because the entire economy was collapsing around them. Strip out the Eugene effect and look at the modern era from Greenspan forward, and the picture is ordinary. Every rolling three-month window in market history carries an average drawdown of roughly 3 to 5 percent anyway. The scary chart is just describing normal market noise dressed up as a Fed-chair curse.[14:35]

The same denominator trick shows up in a second chart that was on screen later. A lot of sectors look like they are sitting at all-time lows relative to the S&P 500, which sounds alarming until you remember that technology is now roughly 39 percent of that index, and almost 59 percent of the NASDAQ-100. Tech has been ripping, so it pulls the whole benchmark up. Financials are not actually falling apart. They just look weak when the thing you divide them by is 40 percent red-hot semiconductors and software. The honest question is whether the sector is weak or whether the denominator is strong, and most of the time lately the answer is the denominator.[44:21]

The crypto angle

This is the exact move that runs through crypto FUD too. A headline that says some token is down 90 percent against Bitcoin, or that an alt is at "all-time lows versus ETH," is the same denominator game. The reference asset is doing the work, and the scary framing hides it. When a stat feels designed to scare you out of a position, check what it is being measured against before you react.
