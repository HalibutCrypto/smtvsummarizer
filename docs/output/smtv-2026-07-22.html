There is no crypto-specific story in today's tape. The desk did not open a token debate this morning.

Where crypto surfaced was in the miner and data-center chatter. $HUT got flagged as the best-in-breed name in a group that has quietly gone from Bitcoin-mining pure-play to what the desk now calls a data-center basket. The data-center trade has now hit the phase where ETF sponsors want to grab the branding. That phase rarely coincides with the start of the run.

Chapter 2 · Lead Story

Anthropic Buys Two Gigawatts of AMD Chips, and Google and Tesla Face the Tape Tonight

The Wall Street Journal broke the $AMD-Anthropic story in the early morning. Anthropic is committing to purchase up to two gigawatts of AMD's latest AI chips. The order is tens of billions of dollars in headline size. AMD is investing five billion dollars in Anthropic in return, the same vendor-customer loop that has become the norm across the AI supply chain. The stock did not run hard on the news, which is a common enough reaction when a deal this size gets pre-leaked and picked apart in the hour before the bell.

The context is that $AMD quietly outperformed the entire semiconductor peer group through the recent correction. The Semiconductor ETF $SMH (a basket dominated by $NVDA, $TSM and $AMD itself) sat at a multi-month top pattern with the biggest names rolling over. AMD went sideways in what looked more like a bull flag. The Anthropic deal is the fundamental catalyst that names the divergence.

Tonight is the first big earnings night of the quarter for the two names retail actually watches. $GOOGL reports after the close. $TSLA reports after the close. Service Now ($NOW) and Texas Instruments ($TXN) round out the calendar. Google and Tesla back-to-back is the largest single-night AI capex readthrough of the quarter, and any single result could pull the software-semis divergence in either direction.

That divergence is the trade the desk has been talking about all summer. The Software ETF $IGV (iShares basket of large-cap application and infrastructure software) is down around twenty percent for the year. $SMH is up roughly seventy-three percent over the same window. The YTD spread peaked around one-hundred-and-three percentage points at the end of June and has narrowed to about ninety-two in July. The narrowing came from chips softening rather than software rallying, which matters for anyone hoping the software side is finally getting off the mat.

Two more names moved on their own overnight. $SMCI gapped higher pre-market on a claim of a sixty-billion-dollar order backlog and guidance to double gross margins. The desk was openly skeptical, given the company's ongoing accounting and disclosure history. And $T (AT&T) opened up around four and a half percent on earnings, a move it almost never makes in either direction on any given day. Micron ($MU) also passed a milestone worth flagging, its market cap crossing into the trillion-dollar tier and now sitting larger than $JPM, $WMT, $V, $AMD and $XOM. It is functionally a Magnificent Eight name now.

Chapter 3 · Commodities

The Inflation Run Broke Out of Oil, and Wheat, Soybean Oil and Natural Gas Joined It

Crude printed a fresh six-week high overnight. The refiners ETF $CRAK (VanEck basket of pure-play oil refiners like Marathon Petroleum and Valero) is at new highs. $XLE, the broad energy sector ETF, is still catching up. The refiners have been the leading edge inside the group since late last year, and the crack spread (the profit margin between what a refiner pays for crude and what it charges for the refined products it sells) is still widening.

What is new is what else is now moving with it. The agriculture ETF $DBA (Invesco basket of soft commodities including wheat, soybeans, corn, coffee, sugar and cocoa) is running. $MOO, the agribusiness ETF, is running with it. On a year-to-date basis, heating oil is up around two hundred percent. Gasoline is up about one hundred. Brent is up around one hundred. Crude is up eighty-four. Wheat is up thirty. Natural gas is up nearly thirty. Soybean oil is up more than fifty. None of those are numbers a trader spends the year expecting from the commodity aisle.

The tie-in the desk kept coming back to was the 30-year Treasury yield. Heating oil, crude and energy stocks tend to move ahead of long-end yields, which tend to move ahead of consumer inflation prints. Yields held up all year even as crude had short pullbacks, which the desk read as the bond market already pricing the second leg of the commodity move. Gold is not part of this leg. It topped in the summer of last year and has traded sideways through the current commodity run. That is the sequence to remember. Gold peaks first, then the rest of the complex takes the baton.

The crypto angle

Bitcoin's underperformance while wheat, natural gas and heating oil rip is a data point people arguing "BTC is digital gold" have to sit with. In the previous energy super cycle from late 2020 into 2021, Bitcoin ran with the commodities. This cycle, so far, it has not. If the commodity leg is really the second one, BTC catching up is the trade that gets watched.
