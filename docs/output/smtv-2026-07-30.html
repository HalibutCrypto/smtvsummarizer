$BTC traded up nearly nine hundred points to $64,780 into the US open. $ETH added ten to $1,918. $SOL just kept trending sideways at $74 with no real move, and total crypto market cap sat at $2.19T, basically flat.

The macro backdrop was quietly friendly. Gold rose almost one percent to $4,135. Copper ripped +2.5% to $6.46, a classic risk-on tell that also plays into the inflation story. The dollar sat flat. That combination normally helps Bitcoin, but the desk barely dwelled on crypto this morning because the entire story was inside earnings.

Chapter 2 · Lead Story

Microsoft Said No to More Capex and Wall Street Voted for the Discipline

$MSFT gapped up sharply pre-market after telling investors it was done raising its AI capex plan. Management said explicitly that spending stays inside prior guidance, that the company remains free-cash-flow positive next year, and that Azure grew +43% to a hundred-billion-dollar annual revenue run rate. Copilot now has 30 million paid users, which the desk noted is more than a lot of standalone AI companies can claim. Every part of that statement was pitched at one company in particular.

That company was Meta. $META did the opposite of everything Microsoft did. It raised capex guidance to roughly $140B, it missed on earnings per share for the first time in about three years, and its free cash flow fell -91% year over year on top of last quarter's swing to outright negative. The stock dropped about ten percent pre-market. Fifteen years of steady free-cash-flow growth got undone in two quarters.

Strazza framed the whole session as a two-step domino. The market is punishing the overspenders, and it just started punishing the companies that supply the overspenders. Once the hyperscalers get punished hard enough in their stock prices, they have to listen. And when they cut capex, the semis lose the buyer that made the last two years of chip revenue possible. Today was semis up and software down in the same tape, which the desk read as the market rehearsing that sequence rather than reversing it.

The bigger read is that AI capex is no longer a free pass. For eighteen months the market rewarded every hyperscaler that raised its spending line, on the assumption that whoever built the biggest cluster won. Microsoft's call last night reframed it. Being free-cash-flow positive next year turned into a differentiator, and Meta's willingness to keep spending stopped being a growth story and started being a solvency question.

The crypto angle

Bitcoin's macro thesis has always leaned on real-asset scarcity into a debt-driven economy. If the market is starting to demand actual cash flow from tech in a period where rates are climbing on their own, that pushes even more capital toward assets whose entire pitch is "there is a fixed supply." Watch how BTC trades relative to Nasdaq in the next few weeks. The de-correlation people have waited two years for tends to show up in exactly this kind of quality-repricing tape.

Chapter 3 · The World Outside the S&P

The Benchmark Is Sideways and Almost Nothing Else Is

The S&P 500 has traded sideways for two months, choppy and inconclusive. Underneath it, breadth has been doing something completely different. The advanced-decline line on the New York Stock Exchange, which counts how many stocks are going up versus down each day, closed at an all-time high on Tuesday. Seventy percent of S&P names sit above their 200-day moving average. Seventy percent above their 50-day. Those are not the numbers of a market falling apart.

The point coming out of the desk today was that the NASDAQ's advanced-decline line has been trending lower since it was launched because the index carries too much junk. The NYSE composite is the read worth trusting, and it says leadership rotated but never left. Big banks like Morgan Stanley and Goldman Sachs, industrials like Caterpillar, defensives like Coca-Cola, all posted charts you would have guessed were semiconductor names if you covered up the ticker. Coke made new all-time highs in July.

Overseas the rotation is louder. Poland, London, and Sweden all printed new all-time highs today. Hong Kong ripped off last month's lows. South Korea got hit as money flowed the other way into mainland China. The dollar sat flat through all of it, which means these are equity flows rather than currency effects.

Consumer discretionary is the mirror image of the S&P puzzle. The large-cap sector $XLY is the worst in America right now, dragged down almost entirely by Amazon and Tesla, the two names that dominate its weighting. Small-cap consumer discretionary is at all-time highs. Hotels and resorts at all-time highs. Cheesecake Factory ripping. Consumer sentiment sits at a record low. The gap between what people say about the economy and where they actually spend money has rarely been this wide.

The through-line, oddly, comes back to rates. The 30-year yield is at the highest level since 2007 despite the Fed sitting still yesterday, and it started rising the day the Fed began cutting rates back in 2024. That is not a Fed-controlled backdrop. It is a market-controlled one, and it is pushing capital into cyclicals, into international, into cash-generative small caps, and out of long-duration mega-cap growth. The tape today makes more sense once that is the frame.

The Bottom Line

  1. Discipline got paid, aggression got punished. $MSFT held its capex line and $META raised spending into a -91% free-cash-flow drop. The hyperscaler that said enough was rewarded, and the market is now openly sorting the group by willingness to stop.
  2. Rates are the story nobody is telling. The 30-year yield is at its highest level since 2007 and it is still climbing even though the Federal Reserve sat still yesterday. Every other move today, big tech, small caps, international, is happening against that backdrop.
  3. The bull market is under the surface. The S&P has chopped sideways for two months but the NYSE advanced-decline line closed at an all-time high this week. Small caps, banks, industrials, hotels, and international indices are all making new highs. A passive index buyer sees stagnation. Anyone scanning sees leadership.
