The total crypto market cap is at 2.14 trillion, the same level it traded in February. Bitcoin is down a percent at ~$62,500, Ethereum at ~$1,675, Solana at ~$66. None of the day's equity bid showed up in crypto. The NDX is pushing thirty thousand, copper is up, oil is down, the dollar is stuck under 101, and BTC is doing none of it.[5:00]

The structural data point that matters more than today's flat tape came out of a Ben Carlson chart pack. The value of stocks owned by Americans under forty has tripled in five years. The share of IRA holders under thirty has doubled in ten years. The 30-to-50 cohort now owns more in stocks than the 65-and-older cohort does. That is the exact demographic that holds digital assets at the highest rate. The kids saving for retirement are the same kids holding the on-chain bag, and the size of that pool keeps growing whether or not the BTC tape moves this week.[10:20]

Add to that the day-trader regime change happening underneath it. The Pattern Day Trader rule, which capped accounts under twenty-five thousand to three round-trip trades a week, was officially killed Monday. Day one of free-fire and a microcap called $INHD went from $3 to $66 and got halted. New single-stock leveraged ETFs keep launching on names that already trade like options. The same retail force that turns up in those tickers turns up in coins.

Apple held its Worldwide Developers Conference on Monday and walked through what was billed as a Siri rebuild. The reveal was thin. Better voice assistant, the ability to converse with it, no live release until later in the year. The LLM doing the work behind it is Google's Gemini. $AAPL was down five percent from the start of the announcement to the open this morning. The rest of the MAG7 had to absorb the move because Apple is the number two weight in the NDX and the S&P, so the other names took the offset and the indices ended up positive.[15:40]

The question on the show was whether you buy Apple here. Both regulars said yes. JC is overweight, added a month ago, and has no plan to trim. Strazza is the same. The frame both leaned on was simple. Apple's capex this year runs around $14 billion. The other megacap-tech names are spending $100 to $200 billion apiece on AI infrastructure. The desk's read is that whoever wins the LLM war (Google, OpenAI, Anthropic, xAI), Apple wins by being the device that war gets fought on top of. Apple sits the spend out and collects the layer.[19:00]

The five-percent move makes the entry cheaper on a name that ran ten weeks straight and over twenty percent in the process. The one risk the desk did flag is the only one that actually breaks Apple. Whatever finally replaces the iPhone as the dominant device in people's lives has to be made by Apple. The watch did not do it. Goggles, glasses, something else, the work has to land at One Apple Park. If somebody else ships that, the layer-on-top trade is over. Until that happens, the chart and the multiple are sitting in the same place they have been on every previous prior dip.

Korean won, March 2009 levels

The Korean won is bottoming at the same level it bottomed in March 2009. That week the S&P printed 666, the generational low of the last cycle. KOSPI was up +8% overnight. Single-stock leveraged Korean ETFs (double SK Hynix, double Samsung) have built ten billion in AUM in names that the desk does not consider liquid enough to support them. If the won holds the line and breaks higher, the Korean semis cohort and the US tech tape that runs on it both get the bid.[9:00]

Underneath the equity tape the dollar is doing its slow work. DXY is stuck just under 101, the level the desk wants to see a clean close above before going long. The cleanest expression of that view is short euro, which is printing a twelve-month distribution top while euro speculators (the big specs, six months ago very long) have rinsed positioning toward flat and are close to flipping bearish. The COT long-minus-short on the dollar came off a decade-record low at the end of 2025 and has flipped bullish. Strong dollar has been an equity-day-down event in this regime, but the desk noted that does not have to keep being true. Plenty of past windows had stocks up alongside the dollar. For crypto, the dollar question is whatever it has always been, which is whether the headwind that ran through spring stays in place.[33:50]

Chapter 3 · The Hurdle

A Picture-Perfect Bull Market That Punishes Anything Less Than Perfect Earnings

The setup the desk kept circling on the back half of the show was the hurdle rate. The bar that companies have to clear to keep stocks moving has gotten much higher this year. Earnings revisions keep climbing, which is the rare picture this late in a cycle. Multiples are expanding alongside that, which means investors are paying up for each dollar of expected earnings. Both of those things look like a sustained bull. The reaction function underneath has tightened all the same.[46:00]

Big Tech is the cleanest place to see it. Stocks have been taking ten- and fifteen-percent hits on slightly soft guidance or a tiny revenue miss. Software is the extreme version of the same pattern. Reports have to be perfect or the tape rips them up. That is what happens when earnings are dragging price along behind them. In most regimes price sniffs out where earnings will land. Right now the desk's read is that the market is waiting for the report to print and rating the move off whatever shows up.[47:00]

The other layer is that the bull is openly hated. Three years in, up double digits, breadth holding, and the dominant retail mood is hostile. Some of that is Trump treating the index as a personal scorecard, which turns off a chunk of the country from cheering anything that is working. Some of it is the wider read that capital is widening its lead over labor in real time, and the people watching their household balance sheet versus their neighbor's portfolio feel they are being left behind. The AI build-out is going to make that gap bigger before it makes it smaller. Until sentiment turns and people are objectively excited about stocks, the desk's read is that the bull has not run out of fuel.

A flat bond tape under all of it tells the same story. 30-year futures are stuck just under 112. There is no message from rates today, no panic bid, no liquidation. The bid is going into equities. The hurdle just means the market is choosing which equities to keep.
