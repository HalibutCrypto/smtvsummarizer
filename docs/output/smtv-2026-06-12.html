SpaceX prices today at $135 a share. The desk's first-tick estimates run from $150 to $170+, and the bullish path lands on a day-one open near a $3 to $4T market cap. That puts a single private company at roughly 10 to 12% of US GDP on the first tick. The desk's read is that another $3 to $4T of supply (OpenAI, Anthropic and the other lined-up private-tech names) is waiting on whether today's book clears cleanly.[48:40]

Strazza opened with a confession he wasn't doing anything different around the print. JC took the other side and called it the biggest deal of his career. The desk landed on a flat fact between the two of them. Speculative growth, large-cap growth and mega-cap tech all rolled over two weeks ago and have been hit pretty hard going in. Going in cooled off is helpful. A frothy tape into the largest IPO ever would be the more dangerous backdrop.[6:19]

Sentiment reads the same. Barron's ran a "too big to buy" cover. A Canadian writer's "how to properly hate Elon" post is going viral on social. The AAII bullish reading is at the lows of 2026, with half of individual investors bearish on equities over the next six months. CNN's fear-and-greed reading flipped into fear. JC's read is that the absence of euphoria is exactly what argues a three-to-four-trillion-dollar first-tick is more in play, because the room is already braced for the opposite outcome.[44:56]

The historical comp set kept circling the conversation. US Steel went public in 1901 and the Dow halved. RCA went public in 1919 and the Dow halved. Ford went public in 1956, peaked on day one, and a bear market followed. Blackstone in 2007 lost 90% and rang the opening bell on the global financial crisis. Facebook lost 65% in year one. Rivian, Uber and Coinbase all peaked on day one or shortly after. The exception the desk kept coming back to was Google in 2004, hated pre-IPO with one article literally headlined "why would someone buy a stock that never goes up." That stock printed straight up out of the open and the negativity unwound. The desk's sentiment read maps to Google. The size and historical comp set map to the other names.[32:30]

The trade plan for anyone playing the open is anchored VWAP from the initial print. That tick becomes the genesis line. You can't own SpaceX below it. Strazza framed the path as faux move, shakeout, then breakout. Fake out right after the IPO into a multi-month shake, then the actual base. The discipline is sized for a print that could rally to a three-trillion cap on day one or fail to clear at all, and nothing past that on the day.[8:45]

Chapter 3 · The Thrust Paradox

A Two-Month Tech Thrust That Should Have Happened at the Lows

JC pulled up a stat on rolling 42-day returns and it shaped the back half of the show. The S&P 500 is up +19.5% over the last 42 trading days. That kind of two-month rate-of-change has shown up only seven times since 1950. The previous seven were 1975, 1991, 1998, 2009, 2020, the April 2025 Liberation Day low and a couple in the 1970s thrust band. Across those seven the index was never lower three months later, never lower six months later and never lower twelve months later. The data says don't get bearish.[48:00]

The complication is that every previous instance was a bear-market rebound off a major low. This one is starting from all-time highs. The two-month rate-of-change in tech is the highest in 25 years and the last comp is the post-dotcom bottom in 2002. South Korea is ripping. Semiconductors are ripping. JC's question is whether thrust signals off all-time highs are reading the same setup or reading exhaustion dressed up as initiation. The data set doesn't have that case.[49:02]

The bull-market-age framing is the cushion. The current run is 3.6 years old, the eighth-longest since World War II. Six of the previous seven post-war bull markets made it to year five and the average length was around eight. The YTD peak-to-trough drawdown on the S&P is -9.1%, well inside the -17.5% midterm-year average. The "age of chaos" Economist cover from April 2025 was followed by the calmest six months in US market history, with the Dow up ten months in a row out of those lows.[54:22]

Underneath the headline averages the rotation is doing the lifting that would carry a thrust-at-highs into a multi-quarter leg if the cycle ages instead of breaking. Banks bid back above an all-time-high anchored VWAP. Transports across freight rail, trucking and the airlines printing fresh highs. Aerospace and defense holding the anchored VWAP off the recent base. Healthcare and pharma setting up cleanly off the AI-as-a-drug-trials-engine thesis. $BRK Berkshire (the Buffett conglomerate, the largest piece of the financials index) at fresh three-month highs. Discretionary names quietly working too, with Cheesecake Factory, Ralph Lauren, Ross Stores and TJ Maxx all at or near all-time highs. The breadth bears the load tech used to.
