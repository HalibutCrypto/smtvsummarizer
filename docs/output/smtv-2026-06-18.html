The crypto equity proxies took the abuse the underlying refused. $MSTR (MicroStrategy, the levered bitcoin balance-sheet play) got smoked again. $BMNR (the BitMiner mining roll-up) did the same. Strazza's read was that the FXI chart of Chinese large-caps, which printed fresh 52-week lows yesterday, looks identical to MSTR and bitcoin on a chart right now. Anything bucketed as a BTC proxy is trading like one of the worst charts in the world.[6:59]

That argument lands in chapter three. For the crypto chapter, the practical takeaway is that until the dollar rolls over, the bid is not showing up.

Yesterday afternoon the new Fed chair, Kevin Warsh, ran his first FOMC. The tape read the statement as hawkish. Tech stocks fell roughly two percent in five minutes. By the close, the selloff had started to fight back. Overnight it reversed completely. This morning the Dow is up +200 to 52,150, the S&P 500 is up almost a full percent to 7,556, the NASDAQ 100 is leading at +1.6%, and the VIX is back at 17 after spiking yesterday. The 30-year bond future is pushing 114 on a quiet bid. The dollar index broke through 100 to a fresh 52-week high, which the desk flagged as by far the biggest deal of the session.[13:50]

The framework JC kept coming back to was that everything happening before the Fed kept happening after the Fed. The curve was flattening, and it flattened more. Two-year yields were climbing, and they climbed more. Biotech was breaking out via $XBI (the SPDR biotech basket), and $XBI ran today. The Mag 7 was underperforming, and it stayed underperforming. Hawkish surprises in the desk's experience tend to break in the direction of the pre-existing trend, which is exactly what played out here.[26:40]

Underneath the headline indices, the rotation is the actual story. The S&P 500 bank index cleared its 2007 great financial crisis highs, the kind of multi-decade base resolution that does not happen on quiet days. $BAC is just starting to take out its 2006 peak. Fifth Third ($FITB), PNC ($PNC), KeyCorp ($KEY), Regions ($RF) and East West Bancorp ($EWBC) all look the same. JC's line of the day was that financials are the heart and soul of every bull market, in the US and around the world, and the global ones look stronger than the US ones right now. Japanese banks via Mitsubishi UFG ($MUFG) printed new highs again this morning. Euro banks have been quiet leadership for three years.[43:00]

Healthcare is the other re-leadership trade behind the print. In all of June only two stocks with a trillion-dollar market cap have made a fresh all-time high. One is $AAPL. The other is $LLY (Eli Lilly, the largest healthcare name on the planet by market value, weight-loss franchise included). $XBI broke out yesterday and ran today. Biogen, Alnylam, NBIX and J&J all printed clean charts behind the move. Transports via $IYT (the iShares basket of US freight and logistics), the modern cousin of the original Dow average that Charles Dow built in 1884, broke out and are retesting their breakout level. $SMH (semis basket) ran another +4% pre-market on top of a +4% recovery yesterday afternoon. The NYSE is closed tomorrow for Juneteenth, no show, so today's bid is also the end of the week.[51:00]

South Korea, the day's curiosity

The 2x SK Hynix leverage ETF on the Hong Kong exchange now holds $13 billion of assets, second-largest of the 250 ETFs listed there. The Kospi fell 17-18% in the first week of June and is already back at all-time highs. It did the same thing in March, dropping 20% in two days and printing fresh highs by April. US investors cannot buy the Hong Kong leverage product directly, but $EWY (the South Korea country ETF, Samsung-heavy) and the DRAM-focused names are the closest available proxy.[3:20]

Chapter 3 · The Framework

Gold, Bitcoin and the Swiss Franc Have One Feature in Common

The dollar index broke through 100 for a fresh 52-week high. Gold dropped -2.6% to $4,260. Silver dropped almost -6% to $66.66. The Swiss franc continued the weakening it started earlier in the week, with dollar-Swiss turning higher in a way the desk thinks is the early signal of a regime shift. Bitcoin sat flat at $64,250 into the strongest equity tape of the year. By mid-show the conversation kept circling one observation. Every asset on that list shares one feature. None of them pay you to hold them.[36:40]

Gold has no yield. Silver has no yield. The Swiss franc trades at the lowest carry of any major currency. Bitcoin pays its holder nothing besides price. In a tape where the dollar has just broken higher and the front end of the curve is firming up, the price for not paying carry has gone up. The implicit message from this market is that an asset needs to either deliver exceptional earnings growth or pay something for the privilege of being held. Anything in the middle is a wasting position right now. That is the punishment going out to every asset class that has been described in store-of-value language across the last fifteen years of central-bank intervention.[37:00]

The Swiss franc is the signal worth tracking most closely. It was the cleanest expression of fifteen years of negative-rate central banking, the place global money parked when it did not want to earn yield. Its weakening through dollar-Swiss is potentially the first market price marking a change in that regime. Gold and bitcoin are the two civilian-facing assets that have been sold in the same store-of-value frame for the last decade. Their joint weakness on the same day the dollar broke out is one coherent message about what the market wants from an asset right now. They are not three separate stories.

The crypto angle

For a bitcoin holder, the framework explains the tape's reluctance to bid even on the friendliest macro day in months. $BTC is being priced today the way silver and the Swiss franc are being priced, as an asset that costs the holder something to carry while the dollar pays them four-and-a-quarter to sit in cash. That re-rating only ends when the dollar tops or the macro shifts the carry math. Watch dollar-Swiss for the early break.
