Chapter 1 · Crypto

Bitcoin tags 80K and the space coils tighter

The chart that matters is the one nobody's looking at.

$BTC tagged $80,000 over the weekend with the kind of Sunday-night volatility crypto hadn't seen since February. That alone isn't a story. The framework underneath it is. [6:26]

$SOL is the cleanest read on the space right now and acts as a proxy for the broader crypto-broker trade (think $COIN). Solana's daily chart died the first week of February and ground sideways for three months, the kind of multi-time-frame compression that rarely shows up on a weekly. Compression that tight resolves into expansion, and the in-market evidence (heavily shorted names ripping, speculative growth back, AI and tech leadership intact) leans the resolution upside. $COIN wants to complete its base in sympathy. $MSTR already has plenty going on. Coinbase reports Thursday. [7:09]

$DOGE finally cracked the 9-to-10-cent range it traded for three straight months. The desk flagged the setup two weeks ago and got the breakout this morning. Hilarious to say out loud, sure, but the chart was right. [8:25]

The other crypto angle today is in the miners. The names that pivoted hard to data-center hosting and AI compute are the ones earning a multiple. $RIOT printed last week and the move came from the data-center revenue line. $CIFR and $WULF sit in similar pivots. The miner index is back above a rising 200-day with stops nearby, which the chartist on the desk read as a buy. [47:23]

Pre-market action in crypto-adjacent names backs the read. $COIN +5%, $CRCL +10%.

Chapter 2 · Markets

GameStop comes for eBay

An unsolicited $125-a-share offer from a $10B company at a $50B target.

$GME made an unsolicited $125-a-share run at $EBAY this morning, cash plus stock, a 20% premium. GameStop already holds a 5% stake and has signalled it will go directly to shareholders if the board pushes back. The math is the funny part. A $10B market cap is swinging at a $50B target, more than five times its own size. [14:01]

The playbook is Amazon and Whole Foods. 1,600 GameStop locations become fulfillment hubs for an e-commerce platform retilted toward collectibles, trading cards, sneakers, and live auctions. The cash hoard funds the deal. The footprint is the moat. [16:23]

Whispers leaked Friday afternoon and the stock ran on the rumor. Short interest sits around 15%, low by GameStop's standards, but days-to-cover is still elevated because volume has collapsed since the original meme-era highs. Either path works for the trade. Deal closes and GameStop gets what it wants. Deal fails and GameStop keeps a stack of cash on the balance sheet. Both resolutions print bid-positive for the stock. [17:48]

The wider tape is backing the bet on heavily shorted, multi-year-base, return-to-growth names. $BB is up 12% this morning on a return-to-growth narrative sitting on a clean inverse-head-and-shoulders. $XRX ripped 35% overnight on its earnings print, which lost 43 cents per share but cleared the bar of "still alive." DRAMM, the memory-chip ETF that opened for trading on April 2 at $27, is at $41 a month later. That is the May 2026 trade. [20:42]

Chapter 3 · Berkshire

Berkshire's first meeting without Buffett at the helm

Greg Abel chairs, $400B sits, Apple still leads.

The first Berkshire annual meeting without Warren Buffett in the chair felt, to the desk, like business as usual with one weird moment. Buffett sat in the audience. Greg Abel ran the questions. The AI deepfake of Warren that played at one point was the only thing that didn't read as a normal Saturday in Omaha. [9:30]

The headline number is still the cash pile. Roughly $400 billion sits unspent, and the central tension of the meeting is what to do with it. Buffett's own answer, paraphrased the next day on TV, was that across a 60-year career maybe five of those years were the fast ones where he was actually buying things. The other 55 were waiting. [10:31]

On the holdings book, $AAPL still leads, with $AXP not far behind at roughly 20% of the book. $BAC and $KO sit around 10% each, with $MCO (Moody's, the credit-rating agency), $V, $CVX, $OXY, and a hefty position in $GOOGL rounding out the rest. The buyback is back on after a stretch of inactivity. [11:23]

Japan was the meeting's victory lap. The 2020-era stakes in the trading houses (Itochu, Marubeni, Mitsubishi, Mitsui, Sumitomo) printed and the desk got to wave them through. For long-term holders the takeaway was simple. Nothing alarming, financials are back above a key technical level, and Berkshire is the largest holding in the $XLF (the financials sector ETF, with banks, asset managers, and insurance as its main weights). For new money looking for somewhere to sit, it is not a bad option here. [13:36]

Chapter 4 · Framework

The eight days that look a lot like 2007

Financials below the 200-day while the S&P prints intraday all-time highs is rare. Eight of the last forty go-rounds were the last two weeks.

A guest chartist on the desk dropped a stat that stuck. Since 1989, only 40 trading days have featured the S&P 500 at an intraday all-time high while the financials sector closed below its 200-day moving average. Eight of those 40 days were the last two weeks. The history of the other 32 is the part that earns the framework chapter. [40:24]

DEC 1999 · JAN 2000

5 days

Financials sagging into the dot-com top while the S&P held the highs. The peak followed within weeks.

OCT 2007

4 days

Financials cracking on early subprime stress while the broad index held all-time highs. The GFC drawdown started shortly after.

AUG · SEP 2020

11 days

SPX printing highs, financials lagging. Double-dip correction followed in September and October before the November vaccine ramp.

APR · MAY 2026

8 days (last two weeks)

The current cluster. Financials below a flattening 200-day while the S&P prints intraday all-time highs.

The supporting tape isn't kind either. 52-week lows last Wednesday came in higher than at the March 30 lows. New highs contracted to roughly 15% of stocks. Over the trailing two weeks the only sectors up besides tech are energy, consumer staples, and utilities. The equal-weight S&P 500 has yet to take out a new high. [50:55]

Counter from the regular chartist on the desk, who reads the tape every day. It feels risk-on. Breakouts are sticking. $NVDA failing its breakout has become a rare event. He isn't dismissing the divergence. He just isn't selling on it. Both reads can be true at once. The framework here is "head on a swivel," and it is worth keeping. [55:34]

The crypto angle

The narrow-breadth windows that preceded the 1999-2000, 2007, and 2020 episodes were marked by risk-asset blow-offs into the top. The tell shows up at or after the high. Crypto getting bid into a narrowing tape is consistent with that pattern. The action is to size what you'd want to be holding through the unwind, then stay long.
