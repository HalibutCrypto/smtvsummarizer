Crypto sat the weekend out. $BTC drifted, $ETH drifted, $SOL held the relative strength JC flagged last week. Strazza's read of the tape was that there was literally nothing to report.

The action was in $MSTR, which ripped on a stack of corporate-policy announcements. A new digital credit capital framework. A USD reserve policy. An update to the Bitcoin monetization program. And a switch from monthly to twice-monthly STRC dividends. The cadence change on STRC is the part the equity market was actually trading. After last week's drawdown in the BTC-treasury proxies, this is the company tightening the bolts on the income story that institutional holders were starting to question.

Bitdeer also signed a colocation lease for a Norway AI data center site. The crypto miners that pivoted into AI compute infrastructure are now writing leases like any other hyperscaler tenant, just with cleaner power and a balance sheet that already knows how to talk about megawatts.

JC threw out a side comment that belongs in this chapter. "Crypto has literally just built the future of traditional financial markets before our very eyes. Literally built it. Showed TradFi how this is done. And now TradFi has been slowly but surely moving on over." His example was tokenization of equities, framed as a fix for the home-country bias that pins international investors to their own underperforming indices. An Australian who wants to own American stocks today still has friction. Tokenized equity rails take that away.

Chapter 2 · The Rotation

Healthcare, Banks and the Baker Brothers Are Doing the Work Tech Used to Do

Three sectors at all-time highs, the A/D line at an all-time high, and a 13F you can copy.

Healthcare made a quiet all-time high last week and nobody talked about it. The S&P bank index closed at an all-time high on Friday too. The Russell 2000 broke a multi-year base, ran a successful retest, and took off. The Russell 2000's two largest sector buckets are biotechs and regional banks, so when Strazza talks about "the bees" being hot, it is shorthand for the two industries actually carrying the small-cap breakout.

Both come with the same secondary fact. Biotech short interest is heavy. Bank short interest is heavy. Both sit at all-time highs. That setup tends to resolve one way.

The desk spent most of the show on Baker Brothers, a hedge fund run by Felix and Julian Baker, two physician-investors who have been the most respected name in biotech allocation for two decades. Strazza's hack is to skip trying to read the clinical-trial readouts and just copy their 13F. The top holdings are $INCY (Incyte, the largest of the big-biotechs), $ACAD (Acadia), $INSM (Insmed), $RVMD (Revolution Medicines), $KYMR (Kymera), Madrigal, and Summit $SMMT. They also own roughly 35% of the float of Kodiak Bio, a smaller name they've been in for years. New stakes since the last filing include Compass (a mushroom-based therapeutics play), AKTS, Gen B, Miram, Evomune and Vermilion. Strazza's whole playbook is to buy ten to fifteen of them, stop out of the ones that fail, and ride the rest. He has been doing it for fifteen years and the trades keep working.

Reading biotech headlines for non-specialists

Strazza's compressed cheat sheet. A biotech either met its primary endpoint or missed it. Met is good. Missed is bad. Everything else in the press release is texture. He's not wrong.

The bigger frame came from the desk's bull market checklist. Six of eight signals are still green. The soft one is global participation, which dropped from the mid-40s to the mid-30s in percentage of world indices above their 50-day moving averages. Underneath the US index, however, breadth is screaming. The S&P 500 advance-decline line closed at an all-time high on Friday. So did the NYSE A/D line, which is half international by composition. 30% of S&P names hit 20-day highs on Friday, one print away from a breadth thrust trigger.

The sector rotation chart goes out of tech and into $XLI (industrials), $XLV (healthcare), and financials. Staples on a relative basis hit fresh all-time lows two weeks ago and a fresh equal-weight all-time low last week, so this is an offensive rotation. Spencer floated the bear case anyway. What if the only sectors outperforming were defensive ones? Strazza answered with the line that mattered most on the tape. "There's nothing defensive about financials hitting all-time highs, bro." That is the tell.

Chapter 3 · Index Plumbing

Comcast Frees NBC Universal, Verizon Gets Dropped From the Dow

Two news beats with the same subtext. The corporate playbook of the 2010s is being closed out in public.

Comcast announced this morning that it is spinning NBC Universal, which is the cable channels, the streaming, and the theme parks, into a separate publicly traded company called Versant. That closes a fifteen-year retreat by all three of the major US telcos from content ownership. AT&T bought DirecTV in 2014 and Time Warner in 2018, then spun WarnerMedia back out in 2022. Verizon bought AOL in 2015 and Yahoo in 2017, then sold the lot off. Comcast bought NBC Universal in 2011 and is now letting it walk.

The 2010s thesis was that owning the pipe was no longer enough and the carriers had to own the content too. Three of them tried it. Three of them concluded vertical integration didn't work and unwound the deals at material losses. Today's spinoff is the final chapter of a strategy that aged about as well as any synergy story tends to.

On the index side, Google takes Verizon's seat in the Dow today. JC's reminder is worth filing. The last time the Dow swapped names, Exxon Mobil rallied roughly 400% in the two years after it was kicked out, and Salesforce, the name that took its seat, is down 40% from where it joined. File that as historical pattern rather than price target. Verizon has been a total-return dog on a price-only chart for fifteen years and a perfectly fine bond substitute when the dividend is reinvested. The 6% yield is the only reason to be in it.

SpaceX is also being added to the NASDAQ-100 at the close on July 6, effective for the Tuesday-the-7th open. Space stocks ripped on the back of two unrelated deals this morning. $IRDM Iridium is being acquired by Rocket Lab, and a separate small-cap satellite name was reported as being in early investment talks with a UAE firm. The whole space complex caught a bid after a rough couple of weeks.
