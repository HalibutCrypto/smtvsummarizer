The morning price read almost got shelved on the show this week as too quiet to bother with. On a crypto Friday the spot tape stays. Bitcoin holds $73,000, Ethereum is just under $2,000, Solana at $81.50, total crypto market cap $2.43 trillion. Most of the action is upstream of those numbers.

Louis Sykes spent his segment walking through what the Depository Trust & Clearing Corporation is launching in July. The DTCC clears more than $100 trillion of US equities, fixed income, and options. Every major broker is a participant. In July it begins a service that lets those participants take existing security entitlements and turn them into tokenized entitlements every day, against everything on the books. That is not a pilot with one bond fund. That is the plumbing of the entire US securities market moving onto blockchain rails on a phased timeline.

Louis framed the why through a hotel deposit analogy. When you check in, your card gets held for an amount you cannot actually spend. The financial system runs on that same idea at the scale of trillions, capital parked against the chance a counterparty fails during the one-day window it takes a trade to settle. Tokenization compresses that window toward zero and frees the float. He called it a tax on humanity that gets paid in fees, and tokenization is the upgrade that finally removes it.

The headlines stacked up on the same morning. CME began 24/7 trading of all crypto products today. Paxos was granted SEC authorization as the first blockchain-native clearing house in the world. Every big bank now has open job listings for directors of tokenization services, digital assets, and crypto strategy. The career pages are leaking the budget before the press releases do.

The portfolio question is which rails. Louis is not bullish on Ethereum or Solana as the network the institutions will use. The argument is twofold. Public chains are public by design, so JP Morgan running its bond book on Ethereum would publish every collateral movement and payroll feed to the world. Second, regulated institutions need privacy modes, compliance modes, and jurisdictional configurations that the existing public chains do not offer. A New Zealand bank does not need what a US money-center bank needs. The networks that win this trade are the ones built to flex around that.

JC's response was the trader's version of the same trade. Skip the white papers, list every company touched by tokenization, sort by relative strength, buy the leaders. Hyperliquid is the name the desk keeps coming back to under that filter, and it has been going up almost every day. The crypto theme is broadening into something larger than crypto. Traditional financial companies adopting these rails now sit in the same universe as the L1s.

Dow futures push 50,850 with Dow 51K in sight for the first time. The S&P sits near 7,600. The NASDAQ 100 is flat at 30,300. The VIX prints below 16 for the first time since January. The fear gauge quietly disappeared while everyone was arguing about Korean memory stocks.

Then there is $DELL. The stock opens up about +35% pre-market. A $200 billion hardware company moving 35 percent in a single session would have been the story of the year a decade ago. This year it shares a slot with $SNOW, which did roughly the same move on a $60 billion cap yesterday, and the desk had already worked through that drill.

Dell guided full-year revenue growth around 60 percent. The company grew revenue between 2 and 7 percent annually pre-COVID and 14 percent last year. AI hardware demand has eaten the income statement. JC bought the stock in early April near $175 and is closing the trade today around $425, up roughly 140 percent in under two months. The 14-day RSI is at 90, the highest reading in the stock's history. The exit is on RSI extreme and on a desire to redeploy into something fresher.

The hardware index that captures this trade was delisted by State Street in June 2020 at the COVID low. The S&P 500 Hardware index is up roughly 30x from that day. The ETF the public did not have access to has been the cleanest expression of the entire move, and JC keeps flagging that the issuers will probably re-list a hardware ETF only after the run is mostly over.

The pivot from here is software. Only 12 percent of $IGV components carry a 14-day RSI above 70, which leaves most of the index well below overbought. The early leaders sit in cyber security ($PANW, $CRWD, $FICO, $GEN) and in a few repurposed Bitcoin miners ($HUT) that now report as compute. $MSFT and $NOW are breaking out clean. $PLTR is off the mat. The SaaS-apocalypse thesis is being treated on the desk as already wrong.

JC Pulls Out the 1970s Gold Script Nobody Is Watching

A correction the gold bulls have forgotten, then a 750 percent rally that priced in everything everyone is worried about now.

Gold sits flat at roughly $4,550 and silver at $75.70. Neither the desk nor anyone they follow is talking about it. Strazza, asked for his read, says he does not think much about gold at all these days. Macro Charts is publishing the same observation about everyone else. That collective shrug is the setup for what JC ran the desk through next.

The frame is direct. The claim is the current environment rhymes with the 1970s. Crude rising, inflation worry, public trust in the government bleeding. Nixon closes the gold window in 1971 and gold runs from $30 to $200 in three years. Most retail at the time concluded gold had already done its move. They were wrong by an order of magnitude.

1971

Nixon shuts the gold window

The US drops the gold standard. Gold runs $30 to $200 over the next three years, a 6.5x move while inflation worry compounds.

1974 to 1976

Twenty-month drawdown nobody remembers

Gold falls 47 percent into August 1976, the day America turns 200. Everyone who chased the first move is out, vowing never again.

1976 to 1980

The 750 percent rally off the forgotten low

Off that bottom, gold runs 750 percent into the 1980 peak. The investors who bought when nobody wanted it owned the trade of the decade.

Run the overlay on the current move. If today rhymes with that 20-month drawdown, gold lows arrive next summer. If the rally that followed in the 1970s repeats from there, gold prints around $2,700 next September and trades above $23,000 by the end of this decade. JC was careful to flag that this will not actually play out that way, and that is the point. The exercise is the shape.

Bob Farrell's rule five sits on top of the whole thing. The public buys the most at the top and the least at the bottom. Right now Strazza says he will be a buyer next summer. Right now nobody is buying. That is the part of the cycle that always feels obvious in hindsight and never in real time.
