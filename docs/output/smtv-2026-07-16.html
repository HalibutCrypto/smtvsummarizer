The chart that opened the show, and then reopened it, is the New York Stock Exchange advance-decline line for common stocks only. It closed yesterday at the highest reading in American stock market history. The advance-decline line is a running tally of how many stocks went up on the day minus how many went down. When it prints a new all-time high, it means the participation under the surface of the indexes is as broad as it has ever been. On the desk's tape, that is a bull-market reading. On the internet, someone will find a way to call it bearish.

The desk cited a study from FINM Group. The median duration between a peak in the New York Stock Exchange advance-decline line and a peak in the S&P 500 is thirty weeks. That data goes back to 1957. In sixty-eight years, only twice has the A/D line peaked in the same month as the S&P. One of those two was COVID, a global-shutdown event that no breadth reading could have telegraphed. The takeaway is that a new A/D high buys the tape a lot of runway before the index itself is at risk.

The companies driving this print are not accidental. Every asset manager and money-center bank on the desk's board is at or near a new all-time high. $JPM at a new high. $GS at a new high. $BLK ran seven percent yesterday, a breakaway gap out of an almost twelve-month downtrend channel. $STT (State Street), $IBKR (Interactive Brokers), and Invesco ($IVZ) all lit up. The desk's dry hook was that if a viewer really believes the market is being manipulated and does not own the companies that would be doing the manipulating, they have missed the entire trade.

The single most interesting statistic in that bucket was Invesco. If a viewer bought the NASDAQ 100 at the COVID lows, they would be up roughly three hundred and thirty percent today. If they instead bought the asset manager that runs the NASDAQ 100 ETF (Invesco, which manages the $QQQ), they would be up almost four hundred percent. Invesco has quietly outperformed the index it packages, and the chart is still in a base. It has not even broken out yet.

The reason the Q's do not feel this way is the semi crack. Since June 5, when the NASDAQ 100 dropped five percent in a single Friday and semis lost seven, three-to-five percent down days in the group have become routine. Semiconductors are a huge weighting and a high-beta weighting, so on any given morning the tape can have sixty to seventy percent of stocks green and the index red because the SMH is dragging on it. That is exactly what is happening in the futures right now. If the SMH loses this little top, another ten to twenty percent lower at the index level and thirty to forty percent in the individual semi names is on the board.

Meanwhile in the IPO carnage

SpaceX closed at its lowest price ever. It is down from a $225 high to the $130s, roughly back at the deal price at which it came public. The desk read is that this is not a disaster, just IPO trading (the volatile weeks after a stock lists while price discovery happens). Cerebras has never closed above its IPO VWAP a single time in its life. The playbook for anyone hoping to chase an Anthropic or OpenAI listing is already on the tape.
