Louis Sykes came on with the strongest set of crypto calls in weeks. He flagged $ETH/$BTC breaking down over the last two weeks, then dropped two structural calls: $ETH and $SOL will underperform the broader crypto asset class for the next three to four years, and $ADA is an "absolute ghost town" generating only ~$300 in weekly revenue against a multi-billion mcap. [45:35]

His frame for normies trying to play the move was simple. Skip the junky alts. Get quality exposure to $BTC and the Bitcoin miners (bases there look ready to break). Last year the US government took a roughly 10% stake in $INTC for ~$9B. That position is worth ~$38B today. The mechanic is simple. Once the government is the buyer of a stock, almost no one else wants to bet against it (you'd be fighting a political and structural buyer), and the chart trends one direction. The recent comparable was $MP (MP Materials, the main US rare-earths producer), which ran the same script a few months ago after government support. JC's frame for the next move is to default to long whenever the government takes a stake in a name. [5:48]

The sympathy bid is already firing across the semi complex. $AMD, $ARM, and $QCOM all +12% on the Intel rip. [1:02:24]

Chapter 3 · The Cycle Tell

JC's IPO Framework Is Flashing Late-Cycle

Three historical comparables. All three preceded major tops.

SpaceX is coming public alongside roughly $1.5T-$1.7T of large private companies queued for the same window. JC's read on what that supply does to a market is grounded in three historical events of comparable scale, and all three resolved the same way.

1901

US Steel formed

JP Morgan combined Carnegie Steel with smaller mills into a single entity worth ~15% of the entire stock market. Dow peaked weeks later and got slaughtered.

1919

RCA consolidation

Asset rollup, not a traditional IPO, but functionally identical in terms of float impact. Two weeks later the Dow peaked and got cut in half.

1956

Ford IPO

Worth roughly 1% of US GDP at debut. The market peaked in April, IPO was January, and the tape fell apart from there.

The framework underneath is heightened liquidity. Companies of that size only come public when they're confident the market can absorb the supply, which is itself a late-cycle signal. JC noted he's not cherry-picking, those are the only three historical analogs at this scale, and the SpaceX wave fits the same template. Worth holding onto. [19:16]

Adjacent signal worth flagging. A brand new ETF focused on memory chips (DRAM, the chips inside every computer, phone, and server) raised $1B in 10 days. Half its weight is just two Korean memory giants, SK Hynix and Samsung. ETFs that get this hot this fast on a single narrow theme have historically shown up near the top of that theme. [13:21]
