$BTC sat flat at $61,250 while the morning's energy went to memory chips. $ETH caught a +1% bid to $1,634, $SOL followed up +1% to $69, and total crypto market cap traded at $2.09 trillion. The desk barely revisited spot all morning. The story for bitcoin holders right now is what is happening to the equity wrappers around the asset.[12:00]

$MSTR (MicroStrategy, the levered bitcoin treasury name that holds roughly $20 billion of BTC on its balance sheet) sat in an 80% drawdown from its late-November 2024 peak. $BMNR (BitMiner Immersion, a smaller crypto-treasury roll-up) sat in a 90% drawdown from last summer's high. JC pulled up January's Forbes magazine cover of Michael Saylor labelled "the Bitcoin alchemist" and Steve Strazza labelled "the volatility virtuoso." The cover timed the top within two months. $MSTR is down another 72% since.[5:54]

The thread the desk kept pulling on was that bitcoin is fine and the wrappers keep selling off anyway. The Three Bs framework discussed in chapter two (biotech, builders and banks bidding on a falling-rates signal) is a clean tailwind for BTC even with the dollar still firm. Total crypto cap parked at $2.09T is back at early-2025 levels, which is uncomfortable for anyone who bought the ETF-ization narrative at the top of last year.

Micron blew the doors off. $MU printed up nearly 20% pre-market on a top-and-bottom-line beat with forward guidance that the desk read as unambiguously strong. The print mattered less than the reaction. Heading into the report the stock had been front-running it to the downside, which usually telegraphs a bad number. Last quarter's reaction was a textbook earnings reversal that took the stock down 30% in the two weeks after the print. This time it crushed it and the chip basket lit up in sympathy.[13:27]

$SNDK (SanDisk, NAND flash storage) ran +16% on someone else's earnings. $WDC (Western Digital, hard drives and SSDs) jumped double digits. $QCOM ripped higher after holding what the team described as its first investor day in years. $AMAT (Applied Materials, semi-equipment) was up +7%.

The futures tape went along. $DJIA up 114 handles at 52,400, on pace for the highest weekly close in the index's history. $SPX up +60 handles at 7,485. $NDX up +2.4% on Micron alone. The 30-year bond future up +0.5% at 114.5. Crude -1.36 under $70. Copper above $6. Gold flat at $4,017. Silver flat at $57.70. $VIX sits near 18, which is unusually high for a tape this close to fresh all-time highs.[11:18]

The footnote no one outside this show is talking about is who is NOT participating. JC walked through fresh charts of the Magnificent Seven hitting new 52-week lows relative to the S&P 500. The "Lag Seven," as he has been calling them since October when a Ron DeSantis tweet about the seven-stocks-going-up narrative marked the top of their outperformance. Russell 2000 and the large-cap value index both printed fresh 52-week highs relative to the same Mag 7 over that same window. Eight months of rotation away from concentration leadership, and the cable-news version of the story is still about how a handful of names carry everything.[2:00]

The other framework on the screen this morning was the Three Bs. Strazza's frame is that biotech, builders and banks all do better when rates are lower, and all three are now ripping at once. Builders joining the biotech-and-banks party is the new piece. Bonds confirming with the 30-year future up half a percent says the cross-asset message is real. $IBB (the iShares biotech ETF, the large-cap biotech index) printed fresh 5-year highs.[12:55]

The crypto angle

A bond market signalling lower rates and a tape rotating breadth into Russell 2000 and value is the friendliest macro backdrop crypto has had since spring. The story showing up in $MSTR and $BMNR is the wrapper trade unwinding, separate from BTC's own price action. Spot bitcoin can keep grinding sideways above $60k with this setup. The treasury proxies remain a different trade than the asset.

Chapter 3 · The Framework

Vibes vs Spending: A Tape Where the Sentiment Polls Stopped Predicting the Register

The morning's most interesting frame came from Jeff Mackey's retail block. The central argument is that the consumer is fine and the sentiment polls are broken. University of Michigan consumer sentiment used to hang around 100. It now sits near 55 and rolls sideways at that level regardless of what happens in the actual economy. Spending data says the opposite of the polls. Prime Day this week is on pace for $27 billion in US online sales, which would be the biggest cyber week of the year. Companies that report continue to print good comps. Carnival Cruise just reported "completely booked" and sold off on the news anyway.[46:53]

The list of fresh highs in the consumer-discretionary basket is long enough to be hard to dismiss. $M (Macy's) at a 52-week high. $VKTX (Viking Holdings, the Minnesota-based luxury river and ocean cruise operator) at an all-time high. $SPG (Simon Property Group, the largest US mall REIT) at an all-time high. $BBWI/$VSCO (Victoria's Secret) comping in the high teens. Aritzia (Canadian premium women's apparel) packed with moms on a weekday. $TGT trades at roughly 15 times earnings while the mannequins in its stores are full again. The equally-weighted consumer discretionary ETF doesn't look bad either, which is what would matter if money started rotating into the group at the index level.[38:08]

The other half of Mackey's frame is the operator-quality split. Wendy's has bounced two days on a Wall Street Bets short squeeze that ballooned the dividend yield to roughly 9% and pulled Nelson Peltz and Trian back into the headlines. The underlying business is badly run, mostly by the franchisees who own most of the stores. Lululemon is selling chairman-Mao-looking business suits while waiting for its new CEO to start in September. Nike just released a Caitlin Clark signature shoe four years after starting to pay her, because management did not want to upset other WNBA players first. All three are management-mistake stories. The consumer is still showing up.[42:11]

The fresh-all-time-high list Sean walked through later in the show rhymed with the Mackey frame. $CAH (Cardinal Health, healthcare distribution) at an all-time high. $ETSY at an all-time high. $WPC (W. P. Carey REIT) at an all-time high. $CFG (Citizens Financial, a US community bank) at an all-time high. Healthcare, retail, REITs and small banks all at fresh highs the same morning Micron is trying to drag the chip basket back to highs. The tape composite reads less like a cyclical buy thesis and more like an execution sort. Aspirational sells. Sentiment lies.[53:31]

The Bottom Line
