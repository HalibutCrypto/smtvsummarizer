Two Chipmakers Cross a Trillion Dollars in One Morning

Micron went from half a trillion to a trillion in 45 days, stock markets from Helsinki to Seoul are printing records, and the desk's only message for everyone still bracing for a top is to stop fighting the tape.

Crypto went nowhere again. $BTC held just under $76,000 flat, $ETH sat near $2,100, and $SOL ticked up about a percent to $84.25. The total market cap is parked at $2.52 trillion, the exact same figure as two months ago. The desk has been trimming crypto exposure since last week and closed a miner position the day before.

The real story is the broken link. For years crypto traded as the high-octane version of tech, and the risk-on dollar tended to find it first. That relationship has snapped. Stocks and the Nasdaq are ripping to fresh records while Bitcoin sits at roughly half the level the desk would have guessed if you told them six months ago that US indices would go vertical. JC put the size in perspective by noting that the entire $2.5 trillion crypto complex is worth about two Microns or half an Nvidia, and that a single IPO coming this summer could be larger than every cryptocurrency that has ever existed combined. The speculative money that used to chase alt rotations is flowing into AI hardware instead.

Strazza gave the one constructive read. Bitcoin alone still looks like it came out of a multi-month base and is retesting that base from above, holding a series of higher highs and higher lows. The rest of the space never managed to reclaim anything and still charts poorly. Bitcoin last printed an all-time high in the first week of October, so this is now a months-long grind rather than a fresh leg lower.

Two companies crossed a trillion dollars in market value on the same morning, and both make memory. $MU (Micron) and SK Hynix are the names. Memory chips are the DRAM and high-bandwidth memory that get stacked next to AI accelerators, the part of the hardware stack that has gone from commodity to bottleneck as data centers scale. Micron went from $500 billion to $1 trillion in about 45 days. For scale, Nvidia took four months to make that same jump, Apple and Berkshire each took six and a half years. Micron still trades around 10 times earnings even after the run, because it is historically a brutal boom-bust stock that swings between fat profits and years of losses. One sell-side analyst just hiked the target from $500 to $1,500 and declared that cyclical pattern over, pricing the stock off a 2029 earnings estimate. Whether or not that holds, it tells you where conviction on AI hardware sits right now.

SK Hynix, the Korean memory maker that sits alongside Samsung, crossed a trillion overnight. $EWY (the iShares South Korea fund) now charts almost like a copy of Micron, which is why Korea keeps coming up as the first market the desk checks in the morning. The framework underneath all of it is an old desk saying, the bigger the base, the higher in space, a line that goes back to the 1940s and just means long consolidations tend to release violent moves. Micron spent late last year building one of the biggest bases in the market before this ran.

The more important point was breadth. This is not just a US tech story. JC ripped through global indices and almost every one is at a record. Germany, the Euro Stoxx 600 emerging from a 25-year base, London with zero technology weight, Italy, Spain, the Nordics, Greece at 15-year highs, Japan, Singapore, China at 10-year highs, and Korea going outright parabolic. The all-world-ex-US fund is at its highest level ever. His read is that if the UK can go vertical with no tech exposure at all, the move cannot be only an AI trade, and technology, energy and financials are all rising around the world at once. Even European banks, which he calls the worst real businesses on the planet, are at records, which he treats as evidence there are no hidden cracks under the financial system. The biggest individual winners, though, still come from the United States roughly nine times out of ten.

The crypto angle

The same AI buildout that just minted two trillion-dollar memory makers is soaking up the marginal speculative dollar. Capital that in a different cycle would have rotated into alts is being pulled into the chips that feed the data centers. Until that AI capex impulse cools, the structural headwind for crypto demand stays in place no matter how good a Bitcoin chart looks.

Chapter 3 · Framework

Everything Is at a Record and the Public Thinks It Is a Trap

Set against all of those highs, mood is on the floor. Consumer sentiment just printed its lowest reading on record, and more than half of American adults now say investing in the stock market is a bad idea. The weekly AAII survey, a long-running poll of retail investors that tracks how many are bullish versus bearish, still shows more bears than bulls. That distance between price and public mood was the day's real tension, and it is the kind of divergence that makes people stand in front of a freight train.

Phil's whole message was about that. He is watching people fight the tape across Twitter and StockTwits every morning, buying puts and shorting strength, and his read is that in roughly nine out of ten cases the reason has more to do with the person than the market. He flipped the famous Keynes line into something sharper for this tape, that the market can stay solvent longer than you can remain irrational. The practical advice was to look in the mirror and ask what you are actually fighting before you keep betting against a market this strong.

JC gave the technical backstop for not fighting it. The transports are breaking out. $IYT (the transportation ETF, which holds railroads, airlines and truckers) is at new all-time highs, and Caterpillar is at a record too. This is the heart of Dow Theory, the century-old idea that a market advance is only confirmed when the companies that move goods and the companies that make them are rising together. When railroads, airlines, truckers and logistics names all break out at once, it says goods are genuinely being shipped and things are genuinely being built, which is the kind of confirmation that comes from real economic activity rather than pure multiple expansion. Whatever you think of valuations, the internals are not flashing the warning a record-low sentiment reading would imply.

The Bottom Line

  1. Two memory makers crossed a trillion dollars in one morning. $MU went from $500B to $1T in about 45 days and SK Hynix joined overnight, with Micron still near 10x earnings. The AI-hardware bid has spread from the GPUs to the chips that feed them.
  2. The melt-up is global and broad. From Frankfurt to London to Seoul, records are everywhere, including a zero-tech UK and a Europe leaving a 25-year base. Breadth this wide is rarely the backdrop for a top forming quietly.
  3. Mood is the mirror image of price. Consumer sentiment is at a record low and retail is still bearish, but transports, railroads and Caterpillar are confirming the advance. Crypto is the one risk asset sitting it out, with $BTC $76,000 and total cap $2.52T flat.
