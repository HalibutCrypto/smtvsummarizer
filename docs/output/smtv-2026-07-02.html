By the time the tape got here, $BTC was up two and a half percent at $61,500, and JC was calling it what it was, a massive failed breakdown that turned into a rip in real time. $ETH caught the same lift at $1,660 +3.5%. When the desk can talk about relative strength in Solana, that is a change from most of 2026.

The Dow was up 250 points pushing 53,000 by the time the payrolls print landed, a fresh record high on the world's most important stock index. The S&P was a third of a percent higher at 7,570, and the NASDAQ 100 was up close to half a percent. NYSE Composite (the broad look at what actually trades on the New York exchange) went out at a record quarterly close. NASDAQ Composite did the same. The S&P just posted its best quarter since COVID.

The catalyst was the June nonfarm payrolls miss. Last month's number was a four-sigma beat, and this month came in soft. Headline unemployment fell a tenth to 4.2%, driven by a drop in participation rather than actual hiring. That reading killed the last eight basis points of hike premium still priced into the next Fed meeting. Fixed income rallied. Gold ran 1.3% to $4,130, silver added 2% to just under $62.

The bull case does not stop at the US border. London's FTSE 350 (the broad UK index rather than just the mega-cap FTSE 100) closed the quarter at a record. Euro Stoxx 600 broke out of a twenty-year base. German DAX printed a fresh quarterly all-time high. European banks specifically are at all-time highs, and JC's frame is straightforward. If the world was actually breaking, European banks would not be leading. KOSPI (South Korea) is back green after being down eight percent yesterday, and even Karachi 100 (Pakistan) hit a four-month high.

The one clear dispersion in the tape is semiconductors. Strazza was taking profits on his semi puts by the open, and his read is that when people start calling themselves memory stock investors, that is usually the top. Software ($IGV, the tech software ETF that owns names like Salesforce, Adobe and Oracle) is picking up the leadership handoff.

The crypto angle

Global ATH tape with a rate-hike premium getting wiped and the dollar under pressure is the exact macro setup Bitcoin usually likes. Today's failed breakdown in $BTC is easier to read against that backdrop than against the crypto-only chart, and it is why $SOL ran hardest into the same wind.

Chapter 3 · The Framework

A Fed Without a Dot Plot Is Starting to Show Up in the Tape

John Netto came on to sketch the second-order story most desks are not watching yet.

The Fed has quietly stepped away from formal forward guidance. The dot plot is no longer the anchor it used to be. That means every data print is doing more work than it used to, because there is no running Fed narrative flattening the surprise before the market gets it.

You could see it in the reaction to this morning's miss. Gold call options traded roughly three times the move that was priced into them ahead of the print. That is a disproportionate reaction relative to the actual beat-or-miss magnitude of the data itself. In a forward-guidance regime, moves like that get absorbed by whatever the last Fed communication was. In a no-guidance regime, the whole volatility surface has to reprice off the raw data instead.

Netto's translation is that rate of change is now the game. Rates themselves probably stay rangebound. He said stocks like the sideways in rates, and the two-month hiccup earlier this quarter has already unwound. But the dispersion around every economic print is going to widen from here, because there is no pre-baked story softening the impact.

The first place a shift like that shows up is the option surface on gold and silver. The S&P headline lags it. Today made the regime change visible for the first time. If future prints keep drawing three-x priced reactions, everyone repricing their vol books becomes the next chapter.
