Chapter 1

Crypto's Coil Tightens, Risk-On Across the Board

Bitcoin reclaims $81K, alts compress to record-tight ranges, and crypto-adjacent equities are already running.

$BTC is back over $81,000, $DOGE already broke out of a multi-month base, and the room thinks today is the day for $ETH and friends. Daily volatility on Solana is sitting at the most compressed reading in the asset's entire history. The setup repeats across $XRP, $BONK, and most majors. A multi-month coil after the early-February flush turned a breakdown look into a triangle. The patterns now need one big green candle in the majors to fire. [18:00]

The crypto-adjacent equity book is already moving. $COIN, $CRCL (Circle, the USDC issuer), $HUT, $RIOT, and $WULF (Wolf, a former Bitcoin miner now pivoting to data-center compute) are all bid. Circle put up a parabolic move on its last print, corrected sideways for weeks, and is running again into the next earnings report. The miners are running too, including the ones loudly insisting they are no longer just miners. [25:09]

$COIN announced a 14% headcount cut this morning. Stocks usually rip on layoff news because the cuts lower operating expenses, and Coinbase is barely down on the print. The more interesting piece is the framing in Brian Armstrong's letter. He explicitly attributes the restructuring to AI rewriting how the company ships code, smaller teams, fewer managers, more player-coaches who can both manage and stay in the weeds. Expect that template to repeat across the industry. [10:05]

The summary from the desk is that every chart touching crypto is currently a buy. That includes the heavily shorted names, the prior leaders, the miners that pivoted, and the equity-side proxies. The risk is that some resolve sideways or lower, and a few do not have the structure to support the trade. The base case is one big green candle in BTC or ETH lights the rest of the space.

Chapter 2

Palantir Just Printed an 85% Revenue Quarter

A bigger growth number than three months ago, on a much bigger base.

$PLTR (Palantir, the data-analytics name selling AI tooling to the US government and large enterprises) reported overnight. Revenue grew 85% year-over-year. Three months ago that number was 70%. Market cap is already around $350B. The growth dropped to the bottom line on high margins. [12:15]

The premarket reaction is a 1% drop, which functionally rounds to nothing. The desk read it as the report being so clean there was nowhere left for the print to surprise. US government revenue grew 84% on its own, and government is now down to roughly 54% of the mix from 60-70% a few quarters back. Commercial is taking over the growth profile.

Trying to value $PLTR on price-to-earnings is the wrong frame here. Revenue growth at this scale, on this size of comp base, does not fit the standard models. Joe Lonsdale, one of the co-founders, was on television this morning saying anyone with the budget to do business with them ought to be a customer. The number under that pitch is the 85%. [13:59]

Chapter 3

The Index Is Becoming One Bet

Semis at 17% of the S&P, NASDAQ-listed names hold the top of the cap stack, and the leveraged-ETF complex keeps stretching.

Semiconductors are now 17% of the S&P 500. That is the fourth time in modern market history that any GICS industry group has cleared 15%. The company list at the top of the index is also dominated by NASDAQ tickers in a way it was not three years ago. Most of today's mega-caps did not even rank top-20 at the time ChatGPT was released.

The other tell is the leveraged single-stock ETF complex. There are roughly 270 levered-long single-stock funds and just over 30 inverse ones, with about $150B in long versus $15B in inverse. That is a 10-to-1 ratio, with the high-water mark earlier this year touching 14-to-1. The skew is a coincident sentiment indicator, and issuers keep launching new products on smaller and smaller underlying market caps. SanDisk, Micron, AXTI, even alt-season trackers got their own 2x products. The 2x alt-season ETF already closed. A 2x Pepe is on the way. SpaceX, OpenAI, and Anthropic are queued for both spot and double exposure. [30:20]

The structural piece is the listing venue. The NASDAQ now holds the majority of the index's top-20. The S&P 500 itself is changing rules to fast-track companies like SpaceX and OpenAI. Anyone modeling "passive flows lift everything" needs to reconsider what is actually getting lifted. The answer is increasingly NASDAQ-listed AI infrastructure on top of an already top-heavy concentration. [42:00]

The crypto angle

The same retail flow that bid up the 2x SanDisks now has 2x DOGE, 2x XRP, and 2x SOL ETFs in the wrapper. The "marriage of TradFi and DeFi" being on display is essentially DEX-level degeneracy with brokerage rails. AUM on these products is worth tracking as a new sentiment gauge for crypto-equity flows.

One last subplot worth flagging from the show. $BUD (Anheuser-Busch InBev, the international Corona rights holder) put up its first organic sales growth in a long stretch. The chart is a six-year base breakout above $80, with prior all-time highs around $130-140 as the upside reference. $STZ (Constellation, US Corona rights) is the opposite setup. $TAP (Molson Coors) and $SAM (Sam Adams) both look more like tops than bottoms. Small note for anyone holding the Staples-side beverages book.

  1. The crypto setup is the cleanest in the room. Volatility compression at decade lows on $SOL, $DOGE already out of base, equities like $COIN and $CRCL already running, macro tailwinds in place. One green candle in BTC or ETH and the space takes off.
