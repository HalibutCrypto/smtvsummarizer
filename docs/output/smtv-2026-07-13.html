The Dow sat flat near all-time highs at 52,850, the S&P leaked a third of a percent to 7,593, and the NASDAQ 100 was the ugly one at 29,700, down a full percent. Under the index level, the semi list read like a small-cap crash. $SNDK and $WDC both down six percent, $MU off five and a half, then a straight run through $NVDA, $AMD, $ASML, $AVGO, $INTC, $ARM, $QCOM, $KLAC, $LRCX, $MRVL, $TXN, $NXPI, and $MPWR, all off three percent or more before the bell.

The direct catalyst was SK Hynix, the South Korean memory maker that supplies the HBM chips Nvidia stacks on top of its accelerators. Hynix printed a -10% session in Korea overnight, its worst day on record, a week after raising $26B in secondary stock. The desk called it “the fourth bear market this week for South Korea,” which is a joke about how the Kospi has been round-tripping every week for a month.

What made the day interesting is that the software leaders ran the other way at the same time. $SHOP up three, $CDNS up 1.7, $WDAY up 1.4, $SNPS and $INTU up 1.2, $ADBE up one. Strazza's read was that this has quietly become the market's default pattern since June. Money is not leaving tech on these big semi down days. It is rotating from semis into software inside the same sector.

The other frame running through the desk was the dollar. Strazza's counter to JC's “bank chart is the most important thing in the world” opener was that the DXY (the dollar index, a basket weighted against the euro, yen, pound, and a few others) has already broken out of a two-year range and is retesting the breakout from above. If it grinds back into last year's highs around 110, risk assets globally start bleeding for real. That trade is already showing up in international equities and commodities, which is why gold and silver both got sold hard this morning even with crude ripping four percent on the ceasefire ending over the weekend.

Bank earnings tomorrow are the first live test the market has faced in a month. JC has been calling the bank chart “the most important chart in the world” going into the print. Everything up to this point has been chart argument. Tomorrow morning it has to survive an actual earnings report. Then $ASML and $JNJ Wednesday, $NFLX Thursday, and the season is fully open.

One breadth thread worth keeping. The bull-market checklist that the show runs on Mondays sits at six of eight green, with two red flags. The breadth thrust still has not triggered. Roughly forty percent of S&P stocks are at 20-day new highs against a fifty-five percent threshold the desk wants to see. Global participation improved to fifty-three percent above their 50-day averages, up from the mid-forties last week. The tape is rotating without thrusting.

Chapter 3 · The Framework

The Winners in the Agentic Economy Are the Ones Clearing the Trades

JC opened the show with a Jeremy Allaire essay. Allaire runs Circle, the company behind the USDC stablecoin. His piece argued that AI agents will spend most of their working hours transacting with other AI agents, and those transactions need a settlement rail that clears in seconds and does not care what time zone the counterparties live in. Stablecoins are the obvious answer.

JC's next move was the interesting part. The bull case in that world is not to own the stablecoins themselves, because the issuers are commodities and their spreads get competed to zero once settlement volume gets big enough to matter. The bull case is to own the layer above them. Whoever clears, custodies, and reconciles those on-chain settlements ends up with the take rate. Bank of New York Mellon on the trad-fi side. Coinbase on the crypto-native side. “The winners are the clearing agents. The ones that win regardless of who wins.”

This is a specific bull thesis on $COIN that has almost nothing to do with the retail trading revenue Coinbase has historically been valued on. The AI capex build-out ends up needing programmable dollars to settle machine-to-machine payments. Coinbase is the primary institutional venue for those dollars in the US. That optionality is not currently in the sell-side multiple.

The crypto angle

$COIN is not being valued as a crypto broker in a sideways market. It is being valued as the settlement rail for whatever the AI infrastructure companies build on top of stablecoins over the next five years. If the agentic economy story lands anywhere close to Allaire's version, the multiple looks obviously wrong today.
