The whole crypto complex is at its weakest level in twenty months. Total market cap dropped under $2 trillion for the first time since the fall of 2024, before any of the moves that defined this cycle. $BTC sits at $59,000 and $ETH at $1,530, both red on the day. $SOL is the green print on the board, up +2.8% with everything else bleeding. That kind of relative strength on a fully red tape is the only thing the desk had to flag intraday.[10:24]

Louis spent most of his segment on tokenization. His read is that the winners of the tokenization race are the old institutions. The crypto-native startups everyone is watching will not be the rails. Bank of New York Mellon ($BK) already custodies $58 trillion in assets and just broke out to all-time highs after twenty flat years. Euroclear is moving stocks and bonds onto a chain. The JSCC, Japan's equivalent of the DTCC, is putting bond settlement onto the Canton network. BNP Paribas, the largest custodian bank in Europe, is front and center on tokenized deposits. Citi and JP Morgan have tokenized deposit products going live next year.[29:05]

Louis also made the case for $CME at multi-month lows. The selloff has been driven by the CFTC approving perpetual futures for Coinbase and Kalshi, which traders have read as a moat threat to the existing exchange. Louis treats this as the most ridiculous setup he has seen. The CME will integrate perps and run them at institutional scale the way it already runs every other futures product. The selloff is a gift.[25:38]

The footnote worth keeping is the Solana print. Total cap at 20-month lows, BTC and ETH down, and SOL still trading green is the kind of intraday tell the desk likes to file away for later. It does not mean anything on its own, but it usually means something later.

The split is the story. The Dow tagged an intraday all-time high on Thursday at 51,999 and closed barely below it. Friday opened with the index off ninety handles at 52,250, the S&P down half a percent at 7,386, and the NASDAQ 100 futures down -1.3% and four hundred handles. Big tech has stopped leading and started bleeding outright. Everything else has stopped caring.[9:00]

Apple is down on a story that it is raising prices, which would normally help the stock. Spencer's read is that the news is a tape-driven excuse. The actual catalyst is that $AAPL is catching down with the rest of its peer group. $NFLX printed fresh 52-week lows yesterday. Strazza spent Thursday morning buying puts on $INTC and $MU because Micron's earnings were a strong beat and the stock still could not make a new high. Most of the best semis went on to new highs in the past few weeks without ever getting overbought on a 14-day RSI, which usually marks tired tape rather than fresh strength.[54:34]

JC dates the Mag Seven peak to October 29 of last year, the day Florida governor Ron DeSantis went on TV calling this "the Mag Seven economy." The group has lagged for almost eight weeks since. Today's tape is the first time in a while that lag has shown up as a clean red day instead of just relative underperformance.[11:24]

Underneath the surface a different market is humming. The S&P bank index broke above its great-financial-crisis highs Thursday for the first time. JC builds the entire bear case off this single chart. Two-thirds of Americans tell pollsters they expect a recession around the corner. Only one in four thinks now is a good time to invest. The wall of worry is intact while the bank index quietly clears 2007.[32:38]

Around the edges the rest of the tape is strange. South Korea's stock market halted -8% overnight, its second historic crash of the week. Crude oil is down another -3.3% and trading under $70. Copper is up half a percent at $6.17. The $VIX sits above 20 with the Dow at an all-time high, an unusual pairing JC asked anyone with a screener to historically back-test.

The crypto angle

The macro backdrop is mixed for bitcoin. Falling oil and a still-bid 30-year bond are friendly. The dollar's been firm and the equity wrappers around BTC are getting their faces ripped off, both unfriendly. Spot can keep grinding inside the $59-65k range with this setup. The treasury proxies are now a fully separate trade from the asset itself.

Chapter 3 · The Sailor Discount

The Bitcoin Treasury Companies Are Now Trading in the Lehman Drawer

Louis pulled the historical comp set for the biggest unrealized losses in the public-company record. Those names belong to a specific decade.

Louis ran through the unrealized losses on the public companies built around hoarding crypto. $MSTR is down 85% from its peak in February 2025, when Forbes put Michael Saylor on its cover. $BMNR (Bitmine) is in the same trade. Combined, the two have lost more than $20 billion in unrealized capital in the last few months alone.[19:58]

That number put Louis in a specific historical drawer. The only public-company comparisons that match the speed and the scale are AIG, Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac, Lehman Brothers, WorldCom, and Enron. That is the company these two crypto-treasury names are now keeping in the historical record.[20:38]

The flywheel built around $MSTR only worked when the stock traded at a premium to its bitcoin holdings. The company raised money against the premium and used the proceeds to buy more BTC, which raised the holdings and supposedly raised the premium. As of this morning the stock trades at 0.66x its net asset value. On diluted shares that is a 34% discount to the bitcoin sitting on the balance sheet. On basic shares it is closer to 39%. The market is now actively pricing the company at well less than the BTC it owns.[39:07]

Louis is not in the bankruptcy camp. He thinks the company survives, underperforms BTC on the way back up, and never restores the wrapper trade. There is no active CDS market on $MSTR, which Louis takes as the bond market quietly agreeing with him so far. Strazza, who called Saylor a scammer two years ago at an advisor conference in Miami back when the stock was 80% higher, agreed. He still trades the name in both directions through the leveraged ETFs ($MSTU and $MSTZ) rather than holding either side long.

The interesting tell is not whether the company makes it. It is the discount. A 34% haircut to spot BTC is the market saying these treasury wrappers do not get to participate in the next leg up the way their shareholders thought they would. The flywheel is now a one-way ratchet pointing down.
