The everything rally rolled into the last day of the quarter and crypto sat it out again. $BTC dropped -2.5% to $58,600, $ETH dropped -3.3% to $1,560, $SOL dropped -3.3% to $72, and total crypto market cap closed at $2.01T, a fresh 52-week low.[13:40]

Spencer pulled up the long-term Bitcoin chart and said out loud what the desk has been chewing on for weeks. He had to check twice that the October 2025 all-time high was only nine months ago. It feels like two years. A normal Bitcoin bear runs at least a year. Strazza's read of the calendar was the one thing he offered about crypto all morning. "Tells me we're halfway done."[15:00]

The only crypto-stock crossover that moved the conversation was Coinbase tokenizing US equities. The desk's framing of who the customer is matters. An international investor with no access to a US brokerage account today can buy $NVDA through a tokenized wrapper tomorrow. JC took the same point in a different direction. The reason foreign indices are concentrated into two or three names is that local capital has nowhere else to go. Tokenization breaks that and routes money toward the deepest pool, which has been the US tape for two generations.[12:00]

X Money launched the same morning with bank-partner accounts that spread cash across FDIC institutions for up to $10 million in coverage. Same theme from a different angle. The bank account, the brokerage account and the on-ramp into US securities are all getting unbundled from the bank.[8:20]

Chapter 3 · The Korea Tape

Korea Doubled in Six Months and the US Is Still the Less Concentrated Index

The single most jarring stat on the show, and the line that ended the "Apple, Microsoft and Nvidia are too big" argument.

The Kospi doubled in the first six months of 2026. It opened the year at 4,214 and is closing H1 at roughly twice that level. JC's framing was that this would be the equivalent of the Dow rallying 50,000 points in six months, or the S&P adding 7,500. And the move is not in some emerging-market frontier name. It is in one of the most important industrial economies on the planet.[20:55]

The asterisk on the move is that the Kospi is effectively two stocks. Samsung and SK Hynix carry the index. Spencer's pushback was that the doubling is really about those two companies more than about the broader Korean economy. JC's counter is the line worth filing. The United States is one of the least concentrated equity markets in the world. The complaint that Apple, Microsoft and Nvidia are too large a share of the S&P 500 does not survive a global comparison. Every other major index is more concentrated than the US one, and Korea is the example that ended the argument.[22:15]

SK Hynix filed to list in the US, which is the second part of the story. The major Asian tech conglomerates have historically kept their primary listings at home. Nintendo. Tencent. Samsung. Hynix breaking with that pattern and bringing a US listing is the same theme that ran through the Crypto chapter. Whoever sits closest to deep dollar liquidity wins, and the rails to get there now include both tokenization and traditional NASDAQ paperwork.[23:20]

European footnote

JC also flagged the DAX on its own absolute breakout, but Strazza killed the trade. On a relative basis against the S&P 500, Europe is a straight line down for years and there is no base in sight. The absolute chart looks fine. The relative chart says uninvestable. Same lesson as the Mag 7 in reverse.
