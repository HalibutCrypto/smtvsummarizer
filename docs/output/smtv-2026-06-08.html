Friday's NASDAQ was down four percent. $AVGO dropped twenty percent in two days. The memory and semis cohort that did all the heavy lifting through the spring got taken out back. Headlines treated it as the start of something. The desk treated it as concentrated leadership unwinding inside a tape that is broadly fine.[2:54]

The S&P 500 fell two and a half percent on the week, ending nine consecutive up weeks. The advance-decline line rose anyway. Financials engulfed a full month of prior trading in one week and closed at the highs, which is the most important sector in the country printing strength into the panic. Real estate quietly notched fresh fifty-two-week highs after two years of going nowhere. Only twenty-four stocks made new two-week lows on Friday. That is the breadth of a rotation. The breadth of a market coming apart looks different.[10:20]

JC pulled out a framework from Ari Wald, the chief technician at Oppenheimer, who tracks what he calls sector culprits. Every meaningful bear market in the dataset has been led by at least one sector down twenty percent. Zero sectors are down over the last twelve months. Without a culprit, the math does not arrive at a bear market. The variant the desk runs alongside is thematic rather than sector-based (a group of stocks across sectors that share a story), and on that read the culprit candidate is the concentrated semis and memory cohort that got hammered last week. Even there, the move is roughly back to two-week-old prices, which is why the new-lows list is short.[7:55]

Chapter 3 · Currencies

The Currency Tape Moved More Than Tech Did and Wired Both Sides of the Move

The Korean won weakened into Thursday and Friday. The Korean-tilted memory ETFs that the US tech tape had been leaning on caught the worst of the equity selloff at the same time. The Chinese yuan dropped with it. The Asian-currency screen and the semis tape were running on the same wire all week, and once you put them side by side the Friday route reads less like a fundamental rerate and more like an FX-driven unwind of a crowded long.

On Monday the yuan reversed. Up +2.2% on the day, which erased everything it gave back Thursday and Friday combined. Two percent moves in a major currency are uncommon. They are the kind of move that pulls the equity tapes that lean on them back with them. The semis are catching that bid this morning. The won has not yet made the same move, which is the watch item for whether this morning's tech bounce holds through the close.[43:34]

The dollar piece sits underneath all of it. The read on the desk's monthly charts segment is that the dollar's recent decline completed a five-wave move down from the top, and the pullback that followed the bounce held the support level the chart had drawn. On that read, the wave-two retracement is done and the dollar is rallying off a major low. The cleanest currency expression of the view is short euro. The yen, which would normally rip on dollar strength, has refused to make new highs against the dollar, which the desk flagged as the odd part of the picture. As a setup, a dollar major low plus a euro short setup is the FX regime that historically pulls global money back into the US tape. It is also the regime that has been hard for Bitcoin all year. The currency tape is the part of last week's story that will keep mattering this week.[41:10]

The crypto angle

The dollar setup is the macro read-through. If the dollar bottomed where the chart says it bottomed and the next leg is up, the BTC headwind that ran through the spring stays in place. The flat tape across BTC, ETH, and SOL through both Friday's risk-off and Monday's risk-on is consistent with that view. The bounce that crypto needs is one where global currencies hold and equity futures hold without the dollar making fresh highs.
