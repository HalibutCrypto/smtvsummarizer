Crypto held quiet on a session where US stock index futures pushed fresh highs. $BTC sits at $79,550, up about a third of a percent. $ETH is flat near $2,260. $SOL hovers around $91. Total crypto market cap reads $2.63T. The macro backdrop is constructive on paper. Bonds are bid (30-year futures up half a percent), the dollar is quiet, gold holds $4,710, and stocks keep printing records.

The story crowding out crypto today is on the equity side. Cerebras is pricing the biggest US IPO since Rivian, and that $50 billion supply event is going to land on a tape already absorbing record daily volume. Retail attention rotates to wherever the loudest chart is, which today is the new listing. If Cerebras opens fifty percent over its issue price and rips, crypto stays where it is. If it breaks the IPO price intraday, capital looks for somewhere else to be, and crypto becomes one of the candidates.

One softer note buried in the macro screen. Silver dropped two and a half percent to $87 on a day everything else risk-on was bid. That's the kind of mixed reading the desk has been calling regime confusion. BTC keeps coiling, and the cross-asset confirmation that would prompt sizing up isn't on the screen today.

The Dow is the headline today. Dow futures printed an all-time high near 50,200, up 400 on the session. The S&P 500 nudged through 7,500, up about a third of a percent. The Nasdaq 100 was the laggard at +0.18%, last of the four major US index futures overnight. The desk flagged that as an unusual handoff. When the Dow leads the Nasdaq this hard, breadth is either widening or money is rotating. Today's screen reads more like rotation (the next chapter walks through why).

The day's biggest news beat is Cerebras pricing its IPO at $185 a share, putting the company at roughly a $50 billion valuation and raising about $5.5 billion. Cerebras builds wafer-scale AI training chips and competes adjacent to Nvidia in the high-end training workload market. ARM holds a stake, which is why the ARM tape moves with the IPO even before the new listing trades. This is the largest US listing since Rivian came public in 2021 at a $66 billion valuation, raised $12 billion, and then fell roughly ninety percent. For context, Uber's 2019 IPO priced the company at $75 billion, raised $8 billion, and drew down about seventy-five percent. Alibaba's $170 billion sits at the top of the US-listing ladder for size. JC's read is consistent with his standing IPO frame. Big listings function as distribution events. VCs sell into oversubscribed books and retail picks up the supply. The book on this one is reportedly ten times covered. The mechanics don't change. The print just gets larger.

Cisco ($CSCO) printed the cleanest microcosm of the regime today. The company posted its best quarter ever, announced a 4,000-employee layoff on the same call, and the stock ripped +16%. That combination is the wage-to-margin handoff that's been quietly powering the bull off the lows. Companies are squeezing bodies, expanding margins, and getting paid by the tape for it. Whether you find that healthy or unhealthy, it's the regime.

One more piece worth flagging from the macro overlay. Macro Charts posted a screen of asset manager positioning in Nasdaq futures, the red line back in historic net long territory. That positioning matches the summer 2025 setup that preceded a six-month grind sideways for tech, where the Nasdaq went nowhere while breadth quietly rotated into small caps and software took real damage. The blue line in the same chart, the QQQ-to-SPY ratio, has started turning down. The desk doesn't read this as a setup to be short tech outright. It reads as a setup where rotation into financials, industrials, and the rest of the index has more room to run than chasing the same megacap basket higher. JP Morgan, Goldman Sachs, and Morgan Stanley all printed fresh all-time highs today even as the XLF-versus-SPX ratio sits at all-time lows. Most relative charts against an index this tech-heavy are going to look broken whether the underlying group is healthy or not.

Chapter 3 · Under the Surface

Five Percent of the Index Hit 52-Week Lows on the Same Day It Hit a Record

Today's session printed a statistic the desk hadn't seen in months. The S&P 500 made fresh all-time highs while five percent of its constituents closed at new 52-week lows on the same day. That dispersion has happened only a handful of times historically, and the most cited instance lined up just before the dot-com top. None of that says today's the top. It does mean the breadth picture under the index isn't matching the headline number.

The names doing the damage are tradeable. Tractor Supply ($TSCO, the rural farm-and-pet retail chain), Cognizant Technology Solutions ($CTSH, IT services), and CoStar Group ($CSGP, the commercial real estate data company) are all sitting on multi-month lows. The desk's three-month-low scanner, which had been mostly empty since the March drawdown, is suddenly populated. That's a soft tell that doesn't make a magazine cover but tends to show up in the right places. Some of the desk's best-performing positions yesterday were shorts on a day the index ripped to new highs.

The relative chart that captures the dynamic best is consumer discretionary versus staples. $XLY (the consumer discretionary ETF, dominated by Amazon, Tesla, and Home Depot) versus $XLP (the staples ETF, dominated by Costco, Walmart, and Procter & Gamble) is the cleanest single read on whether households are feeling rich or cautious. On a market-cap-weighted basis, that ratio is putting in lower highs as the S&P rips. On an equal-weighted basis, which strips out the megacaps and gives a truer read of the underlying tape, the ratio is sitting near 52-week lows.

Stack that next to the cohort cleanup. Goldman's basket of nonprofitable tech (the ten or so names in the compare grid above, plus a long tail) has been leading the broad tape for weeks. Profitless names outperforming profitable names is a late-cycle marker most of the time. Plenty of bull regimes have lived with that condition for a long stretch without resolving. Resolution doesn't have to be tomorrow. The regime is visibly bifurcating now, and the cleanest underperformers are taking real damage while the index itself keeps clearing records.

The Bottom Line

  1. Records up top, fresh lows underneath. The S&P made new ATHs while five percent of its constituents closed at fresh 52-week lows on the same session. That dispersion has historical company near major tops, including the dot-com peak. Doesn't have to mean now. It does mean breadth is the chart that matters more than the headline number.
  2. IPO supply is back. Cerebras prices at $50 billion, the biggest US listing since Rivian's $66 billion print in 2021 (which then fell ninety percent). SpaceX is queued behind it. Treat large new listings as distribution events. Capital that absorbs them gets pulled out of risk somewhere else on the tape.
  3. "Nobody knows anything" is the actual playbook. The seasonal calls and price targets make for good clips. The thing worth carrying out of today is the posture. Do the work, accept that nobody knows what's next, and let positioning do the talking. The tape is splitting and the easy bull narrative gets harder from here.
