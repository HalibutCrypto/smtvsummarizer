The May close ran the same script crypto has been running for ten weeks. Spot was flat. The speculative dollar was somewhere else and it printed in size somewhere else. $ARKK (the Cathie Wood speculative growth ETF that buys unprofitable disruptors and is the cleanest pure read on risk appetite outside crypto) made fresh highs into the close. $IGV ripped twenty percent. Heavily shorted single names ran. None of that bid found $BTC.

The only crypto-specific headline of the morning was MicroStrategy disclosing it sold roughly $2.5 million of Bitcoin, the first sale Saylor's company has made in years and a completely immaterial size on a balance sheet that owns billions. The desk could not work out a clean read for it. Not a hedge, not enough to raise meaningful cash, not enough to signal anything about thesis. The most charitable interpretation that surfaced on air was operational housekeeping. The least charitable was that the company that turned not selling Bitcoin into an identity sold a token amount of Bitcoin.

The structural read on the month is that crypto rails are growing in TradFi even as native crypto prices stall. Teucrium, the commodity-ETF shop that built itself on grains, now runs a 2x XRP ETF (the first US one), a 2x Solana, 2x HYPE, 2x BNB, 2x Dogecoin, and white-labels the $TCAN Canton Network product. The story the desk found genuinely interesting was that the demand for those wrappers in equity accounts is what is showing up, while spot BTC and ETH simply do not move.

The headline for the May close is that software just had its second-best month in the life of the index. The only month that beat it was October 2002, off the bottom of the dot-com bust. If you skip that one print, May 2026 is the largest one-month move in software the chart has on record. The group is also the same one that the desk spent most of last year writing obituaries for under the heading "the SaaS apocalypse," meaning every story about AI eating enterprise software at the operating-margin level. The reversal is sharp enough that Strazza flagged it as the cleanest single signal of the month.

The same pattern shows up one layer up. Large-cap tech as a sector printed back-to-back months that, by the desk's read of the chart, are the top two ever once you remove October 2002. The relative chart that ties it together is $XLK over $SPY, tech sector versus the broad index, breaking out of a base that runs back twenty-five years. Strazza compared the setup to the way semiconductors traded coming out of mid-2023, which is the chart that called the AI leg in NVDA and the whole hardware basket. The implication, in his read, is that tech leadership is at the beginning of a regime rather than the end.

Everything outside that tech complex inherits a denominator problem. With tech this dominant in $SPY, almost every other sector chart against the index looks like it is rolling over even when its own absolute chart is fine. Healthcare is the cleanest example. $XLV on an absolute basis is resting on support that goes back twenty-five years. Outflows have been off the charts. The "everybody hates it, the level is real" setup is exactly the kind the desk usually leans into, but Strazza flagged that anything he has personally tried in large-cap healthcare for the better part of a year has hurt him. The group needs participation to broaden inside it (health plans, instruments, biotech) before he is willing to put it back on size.

The breadth print is the one number that does not fit the rest. The percent of stocks above their 200-day moving average peaked on January 15. The breadth-thrust regime (20-day new highs running over 55%, which historically pulls a year of tailwind behind it) finished in mid-May. Every single trading day last week had more decliners than advancers, even on green days for the index. JC's frame was that he is used to the advance-decline line confirming new highs in the index the way fried chicken pairs with champagne. The way the tape is set up right now, the index is at new highs and the breadth line is not, which is the Kool-Aid pairing. Strazza is constructive on the rotation under the surface. Consumer discretionary versus consumer staples (the cleanest risk-on-versus-risk-off internal in equities) is improving and back into the April highs. The point of agreement between them is that the trend is up, the only trade that has actually paid is tech, and breadth is the watch item for June.

The one fresh capital-allocation print of the morning was Berkshire buying out $TMHC (Taylor Morrison, a US home builder) for $72.50 per share, about $8.5 billion all-in. Homebuilders as a group trade at single-digit P/Es after the post-2022 rate cycle decimated them, and Greg Abel's first significant deal of any size in the bull market lands in the most beaten-up corner of the equity market. The desk's read on it had two sides. Strazza called it textbook Buffett-style value picking. JC's frame was that Berkshire is sitting on the largest cash pile any company has ever held, underperformed the bull, and an eight-and-a-half billion deal does not actually move the needle on a three-hundred-billion-plus cash position. Both reads can be right at the same time and neither changes that Abel just made his first move.

The crypto angle

The single best read on whether risk appetite is in the system right now is whether $ARKK and the heavily-shorted-stocks basket are at new highs. They are. That dollar used to find $BTC first. The May print is the third month in a row where it found speculative TradFi growth before it found crypto. Watch the same dollar's behavior in June; the crypto unlock is downstream of it deciding to widen out.

Chapter 3 · Doers and Talkers

The Hedgers Are Long Bonds and the Analysts Are Short

The single most underweighted print of the morning was the Commitments of Traders positioning in US Treasuries. Commercial hedgers, the institutions with actual physical exposure to bond prices (insurers, primary dealers, pension funds), are net long Treasuries at the largest size ever recorded. On the other side, large speculators, the asset-manager and hedge-fund cohort, are net short Treasuries at the largest size ever recorded. The two groups have never been this far apart on rates, and they sit on opposite ends.

JC's framing for this is the one worth keeping. The hedgers are the doers. They have to hold the underlying. They mark the position daily against a real book. Their incentive is to be right about the price of the thing they are paid to manage. The speculators are the talkers. They write the notes, they appear on TV, they tell their LPs a story, and they are paid on the story as much as on the P&L. Across the available history, the doers tend to be right and the talkers tend to be wrong. That is the structural read. The cyclical read on top of it is that the doers' largest-ever long bet is a bet that rates go lower from here.

The equity tilt that follows from that read shows up in a clean place. If the next move in rates is down, the equities that benefit most are the long-duration speculative growth names whose valuations are most sensitive to the discount rate. $ARKK is at fresh highs. Biotech (a group the desk has had its best individual trades in over the last year) has been ripping in the back half of May. Software at +20% on the month also fits the same template, because the higher the growth duration on a name, the more the discount rate matters. None of these need a Fed cut to print. They need the bond market to keep telling the story it has started telling, and the doers' position says it will.

The second-order read is the one Strazza added on air. Stocks and bonds are now positively correlated, both trending higher together. The desk's note was that most investors alive today have never traded an environment where the two move together for an extended stretch. The textbook 60/40 playbook assumes they offset. When they stop offsetting and start reinforcing, the portfolios that win look different from the ones that won the last cycle. The names that win look different too. The desk is not betting on the cut. The desk is betting on the regime.
