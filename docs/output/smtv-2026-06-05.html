The day's crypto story was a single asset and a single tool. $ZEC (Zcash, a privacy token from the prior cycle that had been quietly bid all year) lost roughly half its market cap overnight after a new Claude release ran an exploit audit on the protocol and surfaced a vulnerability nobody had publicly flagged. The desk's read is that the prompt itself was ordinary, a request to scan the codebase for security holes, and the model came back with a finding that human auditors had missed for years. JC tied the move back to a standing thesis that cybersecurity is the trade you want exposure to as AI gets pointed at the rest of the stack. The Bankless podcast host, an Ethereum maxi who had rotated his entire ETH position into Zcash a few weeks ago, was on the wrong side of it. The Winklevoss twins, also long the token, spent the morning trying to spin the audit as a positive for the protocol.[2:21]

$BTC traded through the wider crypto bid without absorbing the worst of it. Bitcoin held above the February lows at roughly $62,100, down another $1,900 on the morning. $ETH at $1,650 and $SOL at $65.50 both broke below their February lows, the obvious reference on the daily and weekly charts. The level the desk keeps circling on ETH is $1,750. A recovery above that triggers a squeeze setup; anything below it does nothing on the long side. Total crypto market cap printed roughly $2.11T, also hugging the February lows.

Louis's read on the move down was about flows. ETF outflows from $IBIT (BlackRock's spot-Bitcoin ETF, the main institutional vehicle since launch) have tracked Bitcoin lower with near-perfect correlation for weeks. The working theory the desk landed on is that money is rotating to where it gets treated best, and right now that is AI and large-cap software. Michael Saylor posting AI-generated promotional videos about "magic internet money" is not winning back the marginal institutional dollar.[59:09]

Louis on MicroStrategy was the bluntest segment of the morning. The company sold 32 Bitcoin, a tiny number against its position, and $MSTR cracked anyway because the largest shareholders are walking. Louis's frame is that MSTR is a failing capital structure rather than a blow-up risk. Every preferred series the company has issued sits above the common in the claim order, so anyone holding the common is at the bottom of an increasingly long line of senior creditors. He called it a glorified memecoin. BitMine, a comparable BTC-treasury name, reportedly carries an unrealized loss even larger than MicroStrategy's, which would put it at the largest unrealized loss in the history of the American stock market.[61:14]

Louis's broader pitch was orientation rather than picks. The asset class is now wide enough that the selective trader is the one who makes money. He flagged that some tokens are printing all-time highs on a day Bitcoin is bleeding, without naming them on air. The next chapter is for the names riding the tokenization rails the banks just put on a calendar.

The non-farm payrolls release at 8:30 came in 172,000 against an 84,000 consensus, roughly double the expected number, with positive revisions to the prior two months on top. Fed pricing moved with the print. Before the release the market was carrying about 16 basis points of hikes for the rest of the year. Within ten minutes it was trading closer to 20, which puts a full quarter-point hike back on the table. New Fed Chair War's first meeting is next week. Powell is out.[12:08]

The rest of the tape took the data sideways. Gold dropped about 80 basis points to $4,470, silver down -1.7% at $72, the dollar caught a bid against the euro and the yen. Equities did the usual round trip on a strong number, spiked into the print, sold off, settled. The desk's view was that the print is not really the equity story today and that the collateral asset classes around it (fixed income, FX, metals) are doing the heavier lifting.

The equity story today is the dispersion. Yesterday the Dow gained 874 points to a fresh all-time high, up +1.7%. This morning the Dow is flat, the NASDAQ is down -1.4% (about 440 points), and the S&P is splitting the difference at -0.7%. The split between the headline indexes was the widest the desk had seen in months yesterday and persisted into Friday. The KOSPI (the South Korean index where the Samsung and SK Hynix memory complex anchors the weighting) opened down -5.5% and bounced off the lows, with the daily candle setting up as a possible island reversal.

The bond market sitting underneath this is the part the desk kept circling. The ten-year yield has chopped between roughly 450 and 600 basis points for three years, a flat tape inside a range the prior decade would have called wide. The reframing that landed this morning is that the compression itself is now read as a positive signal rather than background noise. A ten-year staying contained at a higher absolute level while equities make new highs is itself a testament to how strong the AI narrative is at pulling global capital into US assets. The S&P 500 absorbing this much money stops being a story about cheap rates, and starts being a story about a single asset class outcompeting everything else on the global menu. A volatility expansion in fixed income when the equity desk has stopped paying attention is the setup the show has flagged for months as the most likely trigger for the next scare.[17:00]

The corporate-action piece from the overnight was the S&P Dow Jones indices saying they will not be fast-tracking SpaceX into the S&P 500. Index inclusion is at least a year away. The market had been speculating that the rules would bend the same way they bent for some private-to-public listings before, and they did not. JC's call is that the window itself creates a product. Somebody launches an "S&P 500 plus SpaceX" basket ETF inside the year to give index allocators the exposure the index withholds. The harder part of the trade is guessing which sector the index assigns the name to when it does land. Industrials and communications were the desk's top two on Kalshi prediction-market odds, with tech a distant third.[9:00]

Chapter 3 · Tokenization

The Tokenization Story Just Got a 2027 Calendar

The headline that landed on the wire during the show was a Wall Street Journal exclusive. JP Morgan, Citigroup, and a group of other major commercial banks are bringing tokenized cash deposits onto The Clearing House for a 2027 rollout. The Clearing House is the private utility US banks use to settle interbank dollar payments and is the closest analog the existing system has to a settlement rail. The DTCC, the central depository the show has tracked for over a year, handles the securities side of the same build-out. Both legs of tokenization, cash and securities, now have institutional commitments and a calendar attached.[63:13]

The pool the banks are pointing at is roughly $100T, of which equities are about three quarters. Louis has been making the same argument for over a year. The tokenization rails get built out by the incumbents rather than around them, and the announcement is consistent with that read. Crypto-native infrastructure (the public chains, the layer-twos, the bridges) ends up as the plumbing, but the consumer interface is a bank account that already exists and a brokerage that already exists. The end state looks closer to "the equity market with a new clearing layer" than to "crypto eats finance."

The cleanest investable read Louis gave on air was orientation. Stop thinking of the crypto trade as Bitcoin, Ethereum, and Solana. The asset class is now wide enough that the selective trader is the one who makes money, and the tokens that ride the tokenization buildout are the names that print all-time highs on days when Bitcoin is bleeding. He did not name them on air. The argument is the orientation, and the names follow if you do the work to find them.[60:08]

The crypto angle

The flip side of an AI ripping through Zcash on the same morning is that the rails that replace these stand-alone tokens are being put on a published calendar by the same banks that already custody equities. Both halves of the crypto trade ran inside one Friday show, an hour apart. The reader who only buys $BTC sees a bad week. The reader who reads the WSJ scoop sees the buildout finally getting committed timelines from the institutions that have to make it real.
