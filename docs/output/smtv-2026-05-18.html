Crypto barely got a mention on a show that opened with bonds and spent most of an hour there. $BTC sits at $77,500, flat, well below the $85,000 level the desk has been waiting on as the breakout that would change the conversation. $ETH is up a percent at $2,150. $SOL is flat at $85.25. Total crypto market cap reads $2.55T and has been compressing for weeks.

The blunt take from the morning was that AI either fixed Bitcoin or got rid of it. The speculative-growth allocation that used to flow into BTC has rotated into AI memory and semis. $MSTR is leading the equity tape lower today, which is consistent with the same drain. The hedgers in the Ethereum DAT and SPAC complex keep dumping into whatever bid shows up, and that supply is part of why $ETH cannot get a clean breakout going.

Until $BTC reclaims $85,000, crypto is not competing for the marginal speculative dollar in this regime. That dollar is going to SK Hynix and Samsung in Korea, to Broadcom and the smaller semi names that have not yet ripped, and into whatever the next data-center buildout supplier is. Macro is constructive on paper. Dollar is quiet, metals are holding, oil is bid above $100. The setup for BTC to participate exists. The participation has not arrived.

Both the S&P 500 and the NASDAQ 100 just printed the highest weekly close in history. Dow futures gave 500 points back on Friday and sit around 49,515 off the lows. VIX is at 18.5, still elevated for a tape pinning records. And the bond market broke. The 40-year Japanese government bond hit its highest yield ever overnight. US 10-year and 30-year futures are resolving the multi-month symmetrical triangles they had been bunched up in, both to the downside. JGBs, German Bunds, and US Treasuries are heading the same way at the same time.

The bigger story is the relationship. Throughout most of JC’s and Strazza’s careers, stocks and bonds traded inversely. Stocks down meant Treasuries up. That broke in 2022. TLT was down 31% the year the S&P was down 18%, and a 60/40 portfolio turned into what Strazza calls a 100 portfolio. Looking at Jim Bianco’s correlation chart, the stock/bond relationship flipped positive that year and has stayed positive across both the one-year and five-year readings. Friday was a fresh data point with both red on the same session. Anyone running risk parity or classic balanced exposure is taking the same lesson from both sides of the book.

There was supposed to be a level where a policy bid showed up. Bank of America’s March note framed it as a Trump put that would arrive if the S&P broke 6,600 with 30-year yields above 5%. Instead, the S&P ran to 7,430 and bonds bled the whole way. The put didn’t arrive because the regime that was supposed to trigger it never showed up. Inflation is sticky. Worst CPI print in three years last week. Crude held above $100 through a two-week ceasefire that the bond tape clearly does not believe will repair Hormuz supply chains anytime soon. CME-implied pricing now has the Fed on hold through early next year, with the next move slightly more likely a hike than a cut. Powell’s last FOMC was Friday. The desk is treating the Fed as off the board for months.

The 1-year breadth-thrust tailwind also ran out. The thrust signal from spring 2025 returned a positive S&P 100 times out of 100 over the next year in the historical sample, averaging 18%. That window expired last week. The desk wasn’t calling for a crash on the back of that. They were just flagging that the tailwind everyone has been long is now behind them. Strazza is working through it by taking profits as targets get hit. He isn’t adding shorts.

Underneath the index headlines, consumer discretionary versus consumer staples on an equal-weighted basis hit the lowest level since June. The market-cap-weighted version looks worse. This is the kind of internal divergence that tends to show up before major equity tops. The signal isn’t subtle. JC also brought up the SpaceX IPO buzz at a rumored $2 trillion valuation, which would be roughly 7% of US GDP for one new listing. Ford at its scale-of-the-economy peak was 1%. Big IPO supply functions as a distribution event, and the issuer side is recognizing demand that the desk is starting to suspect is exuberance.

The crypto angle

If the stock/bond correlation stays positive in a sticky-inflation regime, the diversifier story has to come from somewhere outside the 60/40 envelope. Gold and silver are doing some of that work. The desk has been openly skeptical of $BTC as the diversifier here because of how it has correlated with risk-on equity tape since 2022. The case for a small BTC allocation in a real-money portfolio is exactly this regime, where Treasuries fail at hedging stocks. The case against is that BTC has been failing at it too lately. The setup is open but unconfirmed.

Chapter 3 · Framework

The Kospi Has Become the First Chart the Desk Checks in the Morning

Multiple voices on the show today described the Kospi as the first chart they pull up when they wake. JC was the most explicit about it. He flagged that the Kospi has not been the top of his morning routine in his entire career, and right now it is. The desk used to open with the S&P. That changed at the end of last year. The South Korea tape is the new sentiment thermometer for global risk.

The reason is concentration. The headline South Korea index is roughly 45% SK Hynix and Samsung. The iShares MSCI South Korea ETF ($EWY) carries similar weightings. Those two companies are the global DRAM memory leaders, and DRAM is the binding constraint on AI data-center buildouts in 2026. When the AI tape is healthy, the Kospi rips. When the AI tape cracks, the Kospi cracks first. A few months ago it sold off 20% in two days, came back, and made fresh all-time highs the next month. The candles are enormous. That volatility is exactly what makes it useful as a leading indicator.

The single-country return numbers back this up. $EWY was the top-returning single-country ETF over the trailing 12 months, up roughly 200%. The $SMH semi ETF (which holds Nvidia, TSMC, Broadcom, ASML, and the global semi leaders by market cap) was number two, up 140%. The third-best trade was silver. Those three are mechanically the same trade in different jackets. All three are leveraged to AI compute demand and to the inflation hedge that comes along with the buildout. The Kospi is just the cleanest single-instrument expression of it.

For a reader sitting in Asia time, the overnight Kospi print is now the early read on whether the AI-memory trade is intact. If the Kospi cracks before the S&P does, that is the warning shot for everyone long the AI complex. If it keeps printing fresh highs, the buildout cycle still has runway. The desk is using Kospi as the canary in the same way US traders used to use small caps and high-yield credit. The center of gravity for global risk has moved.
