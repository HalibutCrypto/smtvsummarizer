The CPI print landed exactly where the consensus had it. Year-over-year core at 2.9%, headline at 4.2%, core month-over-month a smidge light. The market reaction was almost entirely positioning noise. Gold spiked $45 off the release on thin liquidity and started giving it back inside the hour. The desk's macro guest called the whole thing run-of-the-mill and said it doesn't alter Fed pricing for the rest of the year. Roughly 60% of one hike is still penciled in. New Fed Chair Walsh has his first meeting in two weeks.[12:00]

The actual story is the second straight day of broad rotation. S&P futures opened down half a percent, NASDAQ a touch worse, VIX at 20 for the first time in two months. The headline indexes look weak. Underneath, 70% of stocks were green yesterday with major averages red. Real estate broke out across the board. Financials, healthcare, staples, industrials and materials are all in the leading quadrant on the daily RRG. Discretionary and tech are the only sectors leaving it.[10:00]

The fund-flow chart that landed on the desk made the same point in dollars. Of the roughly $30B in net sector ETF inflows over the last two and a half months, the entire pile went to tech. Every other sector saw close to nothing. Fund flows are sentiment, not a leading indicator, but they tell you what positioning looks like four trading days into a regime shift. They are part of the reason a 4% XLK relative drop on Friday hit so hard.[11:05]

Strazza bought $CPB Campbell Soup calls into all of this. The stock has 30% short interest, seven days to cover, sits at its 2002 lows, and pays close to a 7% dividend. The last earnings print was the first in four straight quarters that didn't get punished by the tape. Two follow-through days are starting to look like a base breakout. $KMB Kimberly-Clark, the consumer-staples paper and tissue conglomerate, is set up the same way. $MDLZ Mondelez, the Oreo and Cadbury parent, is coiled tight enough that he called it the tightest in the market.[71:30]

The names lighting up outside tech read like a rolodex the desk hasn't opened in a year. $EXEL Exelixis, the cancer-treatment biotech, on a monster monthly base above $50. $MOH Molina Healthcare, the Medicaid managed-care insurer, filling the same volume pocket UnitedHealth filled last month. $LLY Eli Lilly back in play above $1,100. On the bank side, $CUBI Customers Bancorp, $FITB Fifth Third, and $FLG Flagstar (the renamed old NYCB regional) all setting up. $SKT Tanger Outlets, the mall REIT that owns properties across the US, above $38. Even $VBR, Vanguard Small Cap Value (a cheap broad-basket ETF that has underperformed the S&P for 15 straight years), closed at an all-time high yesterday.[42:00]

The structural read on tech is the same one the desk has worked all year. The 26-year base breakout of XLK relative to SPY is real and only one upside target has been hit. Tech reasserts at some point. The open question is how long the correction takes. A three-week pullback looks very different from a six-month one. While the market figures that out, the bid is everywhere except the names that led.

Chapter 3 · Framework

Four Trading Days Is Enough to Change How the Desk Thinks

There's an old framework that says the level of interest rates doesn't really matter for markets. The change in rates does. If rates are pinned high and not moving, you adapt. When they move, you don't. The same framework applies to anything markets price, including which stocks lead.

For roughly two months that leadership was tech, semis, and high-beta growth. Off the March lows the outperformance was historic. Four trading days ago the regime broke. The first crack was Friday's 4% XLK underperformance against the S&P 500, the kind of single-day relative move the desk hasn't seen outside the 2000 top.[20:30]

The mental side of that switch is the part traders underestimate. When predictions multiply, the desk read is that traders are insecure rather than certain. The volume of VIX calls in the chat right now is the tell. The practice when the environment shifts is to step away from the screen. Walk the backyard. Shoot some pool basketball. Think about what you're thinking. The trap is sitting at the turret cycling through bad outcomes while positions you should have closed get worse.[60:10]

The harder skill is admitting the prior setup was wrong and just repositioning without judgment. Strazza was open about it on air. He used to feel like a loser repositioning his book because in his head he had to take the L on every name in it. He calls the unlearning of that a superpower now. The same skill is required when this rotation eventually reverses and tech reasserts. The 26-year base breakout from XLK relative to SPY is still intact and only one upside target has been hit. Switching regimes cleanly pays in both directions.[68:00]
