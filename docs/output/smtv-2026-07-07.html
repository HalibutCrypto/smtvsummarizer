The Dow closed at the highest level in American history yesterday and is up another 225 points this morning, pushing 53,600. Fresh all-time high on the world's most important stock index. The S&P 500 is flat at 7,585. The NASDAQ 100 is down close to a full percent at 29,685. That divergence is the story of the day.

The reason NASDAQ is bleeding is Samsung. Overnight, Samsung reported preliminary Q2 numbers with earnings growth roughly twenty times last year. The stock fell six to seven percent anyway. When a group's news gets that good and the tape refuses to bid, it usually means the crowd is already fully positioned. Semis have run something like 100 percent off the March lows and the market is telling them to take a break. Strazza's read is that the primary trend for the group is fine, but a corrective wave of 30 to 50 percent off the highs would still fit inside that trend, and it will feel worse than it is when it hits.

Apple closes today at a fresh all-time high after two quiet years as the megacap nobody was talking about. Google was bought yesterday looking for the same setup. Tesla is the chart to watch. It has been printing +8% one day and -6% the next, and both Strazza and JC read that kind of intraday volatility as resolution-style action. Both are leaning higher. The MAG7 (Apple, Microsoft, Google, Amazon, Meta, Nvidia, Tesla, the seven megacaps that carried most of the last two years' returns) is bifurcated enough now that treating it as a bloc has stopped meaning anything.

The rest of the tape confirms the record. The NYSE advance/decline line printed an all-time high yesterday. Argentina is acting well. Even the KOSPI (South Korea's benchmark, driven by Samsung and SK Hynix) is off the overnight lows. Gold is flat at $4,177. Silver is off a percent at $61.82. Crude added a percent to $69.25. The one hole in the tape the desk keeps flagging is the discretionary versus staples ratio, which has not confirmed the broader breadth. Getting that ratio to catch up needs Tesla and Amazon to break out. That is the piece to watch this week.

Chapter 3 · The Rotation

Biotech's First Real Breakout Since Nobody Called Themselves a Biotech Investor

A decade-dead group takes out its 2021 highs on the same day the FDA turns on the AI hose.

Biotech quietly did something today it has not done in a decade. IBB, the market-cap-weighted biotech ETF loaded up with the group's biggest names, closed at an all-time high and took out the 2021 and 2022 peaks that had stopped every rally in between. XBI, the equal-weighted version that behaves like a small-cap biotech pure play, is still climbing back to those old highs. History says when the market-cap version cleans out the ceiling first, the equal-weighted version usually catches up and goes further, because the small end is where the surprise winners hide.

JC's frame on this was a memory. From 2011 through 2014, biotech was the tech of its era. People called themselves biotech investors the way people call themselves tech investors today. There were biotech newsletters and biotech analysts. Names ran thousands of percent over five years, blew off in a giant top, and then went dead money for a decade. Nobody has called themselves a biotech investor in years. That is the sentiment reset the group needed to break out cleanly.

The catalyst on top of the reset is that the FDA said this week it is going to lean on AI to accelerate reporting and approvals. AI compresses the discovery timeline on the drug-development side and AI compresses the review timeline on the regulator side, and the two hitting at once is the reason a decade-dead group turns real. The desk has added roughly ten biotech positions across portfolios in the last two weeks and picked up two more CRISPR-adjacent gene-editor names yesterday. If they end up right on the sentiment call, this is the group where letting winners run for years actually works.

The ETF review that ran on the show today put biotech on its Q3 shortlist alongside consumer discretionary (via PEJ, an ETF heavy on hotels, cruise lines and restaurants) and quality mid-caps. Broader ETF flows tell the same rotation story. US equity ETFs pulled in roughly $700B in the first half of 2026, already matching what all of 2024 did. Levered and derivative-based products are the fastest-growing sleeve of the industry, trading around $50B a day, ten times what they moved in 2019. Most of that flow lives on top of large-cap tech and semis, which is exactly why a semi correction from these levels would feel painful even inside a fine primary trend.
