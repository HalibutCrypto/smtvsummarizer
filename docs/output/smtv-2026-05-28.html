Crypto opened down across the board. $BTC sat at $73,270, with the host's phrasing for the week being that the price is "getting the crap kicked out of it." $ETH slid back under $2,000 to roughly $1,985 -2%. $SOL sat at $80, also -2%. Total market cap fell to $2.43 trillion, off the $2.52T plateau that had held for two months.

What makes the print look active rather than quiet is the rest of the tape. Snowflake gapped thirty percent overnight. Drone names doubled in pre-market. Retail earnings printed double-digit gaps in pre-market on a half dozen names. Bitcoin was the one liquid risk asset selling off into all of it. The desk barely touched the prices on air. JC has been trimming crypto exposure for over a week. Strazza's only constructive note is that Bitcoin alone still charts as a base retest from above, and that read is conditional on the next leg not breaking lower.

The mood toward alts was harsher. The host's only crypto comment in the open was that Bitcoin, ETH and SOL are all puking and "Papa Dow" is at fresh highs. No alt narrative was given air time. The flow story for the second straight week is that the speculative dollar that used to find Bitcoin first is finding hardware and short-squeeze names instead.

The first read of the morning was retail earnings. $BBY gapped about ten percent on what the desk called its best quarter in a year and a half. Customers are reportedly buying the new $7,000 RG television sets faster than the company expected, which is not how a broke consumer behaves. $KSS ripped seventeen percent on a beat that was barely better than fine. $DLTR printed eighteen. $TGT spent the morning on the new 52-week high list. None of these companies are running. $KSS is still down roughly forty percent year to date. So is $ANF. So is $LULU. When the market correlates wildly different retail businesses tightly enough to mark them all down forty percent in tandem, it tends to create exactly the kind of asymmetric setup that showed up this morning.

The second print of the day was software. Snowflake reported revenue up 33% year over year, guided to 31% annual growth above its own prior targets, and gapped about thirty percent in pre-market on a sixty-billion-dollar base. A move that size at that size is rare and pulls the company toward a hundred billion. The more interesting read was Salesforce. $CRM was down only one percent on its own number into a tape that has been crushing every other piece of enterprise software for quarters, including ServiceNow, Intuit, Roper, and even Microsoft slightly. The mute reaction reads bullish to Strazza, who has been buying into the cohort and called $TEAM (Atlassian) his favorite of the still-broken charts. Atlassian gapped on earnings already and has been coiling above a base breakout for a couple of weeks, which is the cleanest pattern of the bunch.

Set against all of the gaps, the latest weekly AAII retail sentiment poll, which tracks how many individual investors expect stocks higher or lower over the next six months, still shows 42% bearish. Bears beat bulls for the second straight week. The S&P closes at records on the way up. The desk's running joke is that AAII is mostly "boomer white Republicans from the Midwest" and the methodology is open enough that anyone who joins the organization can vote. The serious point underneath the joke is that this is the same survey that turned bearish at the 2022 bottom and bullish at the 2021 peak. It has been wrong in the direction of the tape for over a year, and it is wrong again.

The crypto angle

Same desk that scaled back crypto last week tracked two prints this morning, retail names ripping on earnings and drone stocks doubling on a government headline. Both pulled the marginal speculative dollar exactly where crypto used to draw it. The risk-on bid is alive and well; it just is not arriving onchain.

Chapter 3 · Orange Swan

The Government Bought a Fifth Sector and the Drones Doubled

Drone names gapped up double digits this morning on a Wall Street Journal report that the federal government plans to take direct equity stakes in the sector. $UMAC (Unusual Machines, a small-cap drone-component maker that closed yesterday under three dollars) ripped roughly thirty-three percent. $AVAV, $KTOS (Kratos, a defense-drone manufacturer), $RCAT, and $NDS all ran with it. The Jedi and DRNZ thematic drone ETFs, which the desk has been long going into the print, popped with the basket.

What makes the day matter is that this is now a pattern with five clear data points. The federal government took an explicit equity stake in Intel earlier this year. Then in $MP (MP Materials, the rare-earth miner). Then in a basket of quantum-computing names. Then in semiconductors broadly through chip-fab subsidies. Drones are the fifth pull on the same lever in roughly twelve months. JC calls each of these announcements an "orange swan," a deliberate riff on the black-swan label, and treats the trade pattern as cockroach theory. When you find one of these in the kitchen, it is never the only one in the building.

The trade structure underneath is concrete. Identify sectors the administration is likely to pick next, get long ahead of the announcement, and avoid going short anything that fits the profile because the gap risk is one-sided and ugly. Sectors already flagged by the desk for the next round of attention include rare earths in their non-MP form, advanced robotics, autonomous defense systems, and any small-cap with a politically visible board. The chat noted before the gap that $UMAC's board reportedly includes a member with direct family ties to the administration, with the dry observation that this is no longer the kind of edge anyone has to dig for.

The bigger point is the cadence. The desk's view is that you can hate the policy or love it without it changing the trade. The next sector chosen will gap. Shorts who try to fade it get washed. Longs positioned ahead will print. The play stops working only when the announcements stop, and the announcements show no sign of slowing.
