The price tape on crypto today held the lows from earlier in the week. $BTC sat near $67,000 flat, $ETH ticked up just under a percent at $1,870, $SOL added two at $75. Total cap is roughly $2.3T. Bitcoin is sitting in a 47% drawdown from its own all-time high while the rest of the risk tape is at or near records.

The pull on the desk this morning was the divergence between the speculative dollar and the asset that should be downstream of it. Over the last month, the software ETF ($IGV) is up roughly 20%. Bitcoin is down 16%. Strazza flagged it as a four-week sample with a small caveat, then said the print is still weird. JC's read was sharper. The crypto treasury complex has erased a historic amount of paper market cap in a short window, with $MSTR and $BMR (BitMine Immersion, the second-largest corporate Bitcoin holder) sitting on roughly $11B in unrealized losses on their holdings. JC called it some of the worst capital destruction in American corporate history. Strazza pushed back on the word "losses" because the names have not realized anything, and reminded the desk that $MSTR was the best stock in the S&P 500 a year ago. Same company, same playbook, opposite print on Bitcoin.

The thesis Strazza kept coming back to is the inner-market one. Every risk-on corner is leading right now. Space stocks, drone stocks, data-center stocks, anything heavily shorted, anything with an animal in the name. He said it would shock him for the bull tape to play out fully while Bitcoin sits at lows the whole way. The frame the desk is running is to buy the regret trade in a small permanent slice, treat it like another Mag-7 component, and let the speculative dollar find it on its own clock.

The Dow closed yesterday at the highest print in its history at $51,170, then traded down about 230 points on the day. Strazza's read on the bar was that it is the first fresh ATH on the Dow since January, and that the breakout is meaningful after five months of chop. The S&P sits around $7,600 with the desk noting the index added a quick thousand points to get here. NDX traded near $30,730. VIX held just above 16. JC's framing question on the bar was whether the chart looked like the end of an exhausted trend or the beginning of a new one. Strazza's answer was no signs of exhaustion, going higher, baby.

The same chart pattern is showing up outside the US. Singapore broke out of a seventeen-year base to new all-time highs. The driver inside the index is the banking sector, which is roughly half the weight. The desk had to wave off the question of whether AI was doing the work, since the index has almost no tech in it. The same multi-decade base pattern is showing up across Asian bank sectors more broadly.

SpaceX got another data point overnight. Reuters reported the IPO is being priced near $1.75T. Total global stock market cap just crossed $150T. The deal would represent over one percent of every listed equity in the world arriving in a single offering. JC ran the math live and let the implication sit. The desk's pattern read on the recent IPO wave is that day-one almost always spikes, then the open float has to absorb venture-capital supply for months before the chart bases out.

Chapter 3 · Framework

The 1999 Analog Doesn't Carry on the PE Tape

The chart that ate the second segment was a 1999-era table of PE multiples on the marquee tech names of that cycle. JDS Uniphase north of 200x trailing. Cisco, Yahoo, Sun in the same neighborhood. The point of the table was that the math which defined nineteen ninety-nine does not show up in 2026 numbers.

2026 Forward PE
  • $MU Micron · ~10.5x
  • $NVDA Nvidia · ~25.4x
  • Hot-name cohort · single to low-double digits
2000 Trailing PE
  • JDS Uniphase · >200x
  • Cisco, Yahoo, Sun · 100-200x range
  • Speculative cohort · earnings basically zero

Pulled forward to today, $MU (Micron, the largest US memory-chip maker) is trading at a forward PE of roughly 10.5x. $NVDA is at 25.4x. The high-flyer cohort of the current cycle is trading at single-digit and low-double-digit multiples on forward earnings. JDSU and Cisco at the same point in their own cycle were five to twenty times higher than that. The bears keep reaching for the dot-com analog because the speculation feels familiar. The PE tape will not let them land it.

The IPO drawdown story is the other half of the picture, and it is real. The desk ran a Truist table from Keith Lerner showing one-year max drawdowns on the biggest IPOs of the last fifteen years. Almost every one of them is deep in the red at some point inside year one. JC's read was that this is venture capital dumping bags on retail and retail lining up to take them. The caveat in the chat was that many of those IPOs did V-recover. JC's response was that the bag-holding window is the point. Wait six to twelve months and the entry is better.

The vocabulary of 2026 is showing up around the same pattern. The desk noted the new market words this year are "photonics" and "neocloud," the way "subprime mortgage" was the new word of 2006. $GLW (Corning, the legacy photonics-grade fiber name) ran from $100 in February to $200 now. Tuttle Capital just launched a photonics ETF. Cluster ETF launches against a hot vertical have historically been a late-cycle tell.
