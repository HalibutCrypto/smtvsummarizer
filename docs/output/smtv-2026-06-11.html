The headline tape opened mildly green and slightly tilted toward the cohorts the desk has been chasing for two weeks. S&P up a third, NDX and Russell tied at +0.5%, Dow up a third. Yesterday's session was the second straight day of red indexes and green breadth. Industrials closed -3%. Transports closed -3%. JC pulled up every name in the Dow Jones Transportation Average. All 20 closed at the highest levels they have ever printed. $MATX Matson and $LSTR Landstar (the big container shippers) at all-time highs. $R Ryder, the heavy-truck leasing operator, new highs. $CSX, $NSC Norfolk Southern, $UNP Union Pacific, $JBHT JB Hunt and $CHRW CH Robinson, freight rail and trucking logistics, all-time highs across the line. $ODFL Old Dominion, the cleanest stock in the trucking sleeve, breaking out of a base. $DAL Delta and $UAL United (two airlines the desk has spent ten years calling the worst businesses in the world) coming out of 10-year bases. Even $LUV Southwest, $AAL American and $ALK Alaska are basing. $CAR Avis squeezed. $UPS bottomed exactly at its COVID low. $FDX FedEx, $EXPD Expeditors and $KEX Kirby, all on base breakouts. As JC put it, when even the airlines look good, that is a bullish signal.[21:50]

The community-bank index broke a multi-year base to a fresh all-time high in the same tape. This is the group that was the headline trauma of the 2023 regional-bank scare. The chart is moving in the opposite direction from a world that is ending. Sentiment hasn't caught up. The AAII bull-bear survey hit the fewest bulls of 2026 this week and half of individual investors are bearish six months out. The desk's framing was simple. The data isn't broken. People are angry.[19:30]

Outside transports and banks, the rotation hit the consumer line all week. $CBRL Cracker Barrel up +30%. $CASY Casey's General Stores, the Iowa gas-station chain that quietly sells more pizza than most pizza chains, guided to the upside on a pizza beat. $ELY Callaway, the golf-club maker, up 7% on the week after finally spinning off the unloved Topgolf range business at what looked like a fire-sale price. $LOVE Lovesac, the modular-couch operator that puts subwoofers in seat cushions, beat earnings handily. $IMAX moving for the first time in a year. $SEXY, the renamed Victoria's Secret, printed 13 to 14% comp-store-sales growth into 18% short interest with smart money still leaning the wrong way. None of these stocks was supposed to be working. All of them are working.[32:00]

The relative rotation graph behind the move tells the story in one chart. On the daily timeframe, tech is the only sector still in the leading quadrant and it is pointed straight at lagging. Everything else is heading toward improving and leading. On the weekly timeframe tech is still the leader, but the rotation runs clockwise. If the weekly hooks the way the daily already has, the breadth trade has another quarter in it. Strazza's read was that tech is taking a breather and digesting gains while the rest of the tape gets a turn.[26:30]

The crypto angle

If the breadth trade is real, BTC's range-bound tape may be a function of the same fund flows that kept tech in the leading quadrant for 30 months. The same ~$30B in ETF inflows that piled into $XLK over the spring is the order book that left crypto. A rotation back toward improving sectors that includes crypto rather than the new winners would be the bullish read here. The early daily RRG hooks suggest it hasn't started yet.

Chapter 3 · The IPO

Tomorrow's IPO Is Either a Three-Trillion-Dollar Print or No Print at All

SpaceX prices its IPO Friday afternoon. The deal is reportedly four times oversubscribed. The desk's base case is that it opens. The desk's tail case is that it doesn't. Both are live outcomes, and the show is going live at 2pm Eastern to cover whichever one shows up.[7:53]

The size is the thing. The book is sized around $2 trillion. If oversubscription holds and the indexers move into it, the day-one print could be closer to $3 or even $3.5 trillion. JC's read was that the high of the open is likely the high for the year. He thinks it gaps up, sells off, and a six-to-nine-month retail-bag-holder window starts before the eventual move higher into 2027. Strazza framed it the same way. The underwriters have spent six months making sure this thing prints cleanly. The index providers have changed rules to accommodate a single company this size. The order book is built on demand four times the supply. Something is going to print.[9:50]

The other tail is that it doesn't open at all on Friday. The desk's discussion was unusually frank on this point. A $2T IPO opening on a Friday afternoon in the summer is a clearing problem before it is a price problem. If the deal can't open, the equity tape probably reacts hard to the downside intraday and the show wants to be live to fade the dip. That is what the 2pm Eastern slot is for. Almost the entire bench will be on the stream.[15:20]

The smaller space-stock cohort has already been running this trade out. Most of the listed pure-play space names are down 30 to 50% from recent highs. The desk is split on whether the IPO lifts them with a sector tide or marks the cycle top in space. JC bought one on a pullback and has it on a tight leash with size he can cut intraday if the IPO fades. The position is open.[27:50]

The longer-running point the show keeps coming back to is that space-adjacent capex has historically been a graveyard for shareholders. Boeing's long-term chart is the cleanest reminder. The trade the desk is willing to take is the IPO event itself, sized for a tape that could rally trillions of dollars on day one or refuse to clear at all. Anything past that, the desk wants nothing to do with on day one.

Local angle · PHT

For Manila timezone, the SpaceX open is the small hours of Saturday morning, June 13, PHT. The show's 2pm Eastern live stream lands roughly 2am PHT Saturday. If the open is the high of the year as JC suggested, the window to sell into strength is overnight in Asia hours. The deeper dip-buy is the dip nobody can buy unless they are awake.
