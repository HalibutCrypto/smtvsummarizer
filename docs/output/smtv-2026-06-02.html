Gold up one percent, silver up two, copper printing fresh all-time highs, software in the middle of its second-best month on record, equal-weight S&P at new highs, banks rotating up. Every speculative dollar that walked into the building today found something. None of it found crypto.

Spencer's frame for owning Bitcoin in this stretch came up early and stuck around. Buy it for the regret-minimization reason, treat it like Apple or any other Mag 7 component, then forget about it for a couple of years. The argument is not that the next month is friendly. The argument is that the asset's tail outcomes are still wide enough to justify a small permanent allocation that the holder agrees in advance not to look at.

The breaking print of the morning was Alphabet announcing an eighty-billion-dollar secondary offering. Thirty billion in convertible preferred, forty billion in at-the-money common stock, and a ten-billion-dollar private placement set aside for Berkshire Hathaway. The desk's read of the size is that this is the largest secondary on record and that the ten-billion private placement alone is the largest single private placement Strazza could think of off the cuff. Alphabet also created a new class C share class on the way through, because there were not enough existing class C shares to accommodate the deal.

The ten-billion check is now Berkshire's largest single equity position by a wide margin, ahead of what is left of the Apple stake. The Apple comparison is the one worth holding onto. Berkshire's reputation is built on cash-flow-and-factory value investing, and the single best move they ever made in dollar terms was holding a megacap tech stock for a long time. Trying to run that playbook back with the safest megacap on the modern list is exactly what a ten-billion private placement into Google looks like.

The desk's secondary read is that ten billion does not actually move the needle for Berkshire. The cash pile is the largest any company has ever held. To make a position that registers, Berkshire has to write a check that is large enough to require a custom share class and a private placement, and even then the slice is only a small fraction of the cash on the balance sheet. JC's framing was that the size constraint is its own structural disadvantage, and the individual investor's ability to make an oversized concentrated bet on something nobody else has to know about is the structural edge that goes with being small.

The other live story across the morning was the S&P index committee reportedly debating whether to waive its profitability requirement so it can let SpaceX into the index inside the next few weeks. The SpaceX listing is two weeks out. NASDAQ has already updated its rules so a name that clears the top-tier market cap threshold (roughly eighteen billion, easily cleared) enters five trading days after listing. The Russell 1000 admits the same way. The S&P is the holdout. Strazza's read on it is that the S&P has historically been the index with rules and that bending them here is the wrong call, since the rule was not bent for Palantir or Nvidia. JC's read is that there are no rules left in 2026 indexing, and the competitive pressure from a NASDAQ that gets to claim it owns every new megacap is what is pushing the committee toward a change.

A separate single-name story sat alongside the index debate. Jensen Huang appeared on stage at a Marvell event in Taiwan and called the company the next trillion-dollar name. $MRVL added roughly +20% on the day, somewhere near $200B in market cap from a single quote. The desk's note on it was that single-session moves of five to ten percent should not be normal for stocks of this size and have become normal anyway. $ORCL ran $180 to $250 in three trading days. Marvell ran twenty percent in a session. The pattern keeps showing up.

JC read the morning tape and glossed past copper at fresh all-time highs without a second beat. Spencer pulled him back to it. Industrial-metal stock charts as a group are sitting on the longest, cleanest, multi-decade bases anywhere in the market right now. $FCX (Freeport-McMoRan, the largest US copper miner), $CSTM (Constellium, aluminum), $CNX (CNX Resources, just came out of a base of its own), and a row of smaller names underneath are coming out of patterns the desk says go back a decade or longer. Several of them just broke out together.

Strazza's analog for what the patterns look like is the semiconductor sector before the dot-com bubble. Same multi-decade coiled bases, same dispersion across the group, same setup of a sector the desk and its callers stopped paying attention to during the years it stayed quiet. The semi group ran for the better part of two decades after those bases broke. JC's frame is the same. These are leap patterns. Get on board, hold on, and let the regime run. The base trade pairs with the inside-the-group divergence Strazza ran live on air, where the industrial-metals composite versus the precious-metals composite ($XME divided by $GDX) sits at fresh fifty-two-week highs. Industrial is leading precious right now even with gold and silver also up on the day.

The supply side reinforces the chart read. Copper has a structural shortage that will not resolve without a new technology for moving electricity, which keeps every dip bid. Demand from the grid electrification cycle, the data-center build, the EV transition, and the rare-earth scramble all compound on the same small handful of names. The orange-swan capital that has been buying rare-earth and metals stocks at the government level ($MP Materials, $USAR, $TMQ Resources, Lithium Americas, and a few others rumored to be inside the basket) adds new flow on top of names whose market caps are small enough that any incremental dollar moves the chart. Standing short any of these into a government bid is the kind of trade the desk is openly warning against.

The crypto angle

The crypto angle today is the asset that did not catch the bid. Risk appetite is in the system. The speculative dollar found software, found metals, found a chip-design name on a CEO quote, found a private placement worth bragging about, and walked past Bitcoin on the way. The historical relationship the desk leans on is that the speculative dollar eventually widens out and finds crypto last. The metals breakout is one of the cleanest signals that tide is in.
