What the desk kept saying was that today is a reaction trade rather than a positioning trade. Jim Bianco's note this morning is the framework the desk latched onto. Since the financial crisis, the Fed has held 135 FOMC meetings, and only four of them have gone into decision day with hike or cut odds stuck between 33 percent and 66 percent in the preceding days. That is roughly 3 percent of the sample. The market almost always figures it out before Powell shows up. Fed fund futures typically compress to 80, 90, 99 percent in the days leading in. This week has done the opposite. Odds have widened rather than tightened. The last time this happened was September 2024, the surprise 50 basis point cut meeting. Before that, three others clustered around COVID and the 2019 hiking cycle.

What that means for today is that the market is genuinely on both sides of this trade. Fed funds futures are pricing about a 35 percent probability of a hike right now and something like 95 percent by September. So the market has already effectively baked in one hike over the next two months. What it cannot decide is whether that hike is today or seven weeks from now.

Strazza's read is that the risk is skewed dovish. If Powell holds and signals no imminent hike, the September curve unwinds, priced-in hike premium comes out of risk assets, and the market ramps. If he actually hikes today, home builders get rocked, banks give back their recent breakout, speculative growth takes a hit, biotech looks less pretty, and crypto potentially falls off a cliff. Either way, the desk expects at least two reversals between 2:00 and 2:45 pm ET while the tape processes the statement, the press conference, and whatever Powell says off script.

The Fed print is not the only event tonight. Meta and Microsoft report after the close, and Apple and Amazon go tomorrow evening. That is roughly fifteen trillion dollars of market capitalization delivering on the same 24 hours. Strazza's tell for the software reaction is ServiceNow, which knee-jerked lower on its own earnings last week and has since ripped roughly 20 percent in three days, reclaiming the VWAP from the June high and the VWAP from the April low in the same session. For the past six quarters, ServiceNow's post-earnings drift has told the desk which way to lean on Microsoft. Right now it says be constructive.

Chapter 3 · Framework

Goldman's Momentum Basket Just Printed Its Worst Month in History and Apple's Frugality Is the Mirror Image

The chart of the day is not a chart of the S&P or the NASDAQ. It is Goldman Sachs's high-momentum basket, on pace for its single worst month in history. Worse than COVID. Worse than the financial crisis. Worse than the dotcom bust. The liquid retail proxy for that basket, $MTUM, is down about 15 percent in the past four weeks. Not a slow twelve-month grind like 2022. A four-week face-rip.

The show's canonical example of what that looks like at the single-stock level is SanDisk. This stock went from $30 a share in April of last year to over $2,300 a share by earlier this month. Roughly an +8,000% rally in a little over a year. Yesterday alone it printed a −14% session, and its peers all posted double-digit losses in sympathy. Over the last 21 trading days SanDisk is down more than −55%. Strazza's frame for what is happening is that this is not the trend breaking. This is how a parabolic trend cools off. Things that go up fast also come down fast.

The map he laid out on $SMH makes the point in cleaner form. Semis have already broken below the VWAP from the March lows this year. The next real support the desk is watching is the VWAP from the April 2024 lows, which sits near $390, roughly 20-25% below current levels. SMH could fall another quarter from here and still carve out a higher low against last year's base. In other words, semiconductors as a group can complete this top, put in a 40-50 percent peak-to-trough drawdown from the June top, and the primary uptrend from last year's lows would remain fully intact. Zoom out on the chart and the trend is still there. The requirement is patience and a long enough time horizon that a 50 percent drawdown does not force you out.

SK Hynix reporting last night is the same story in one line. Revenue up 500 percent and it still missed. When a stock has been priced for perfection for six straight quarters, a 500 percent revenue number that undershoots by a nose is enough to send it lower and to knock the KOSPI down six percent with it. EWY is off roughly 35 percent from its recent highs. That is not a Korea story. That is the same momentum basket unwinding on a different exchange.

The mirror image of all this is Apple. Apple relative to Microsoft just broke out to its highest level since 1989. Apple versus the equal-weighted Magnificent 7 ETF is at fresh 52-week highs. Apple versus the NASDAQ 100 is clearing a multi-year base. That relative trend turned in July-August of last year. The mechanical explanation the desk gave is simple. For most of the past three years, the market rewarded the Mag 7 names that spent aggressively on AI capex. Meta, Google, Amazon all lit money on fire to build out data centers and got paid for it. That has now flipped. Free cash flow is turning negative for the ones that spend, and the market has stopped applauding. Apple, which spends far less on AI infrastructure and returns cash to shareholders instead, is the beneficiary. The regime shift and the momentum basket unwind are the same trade seen from two angles.
