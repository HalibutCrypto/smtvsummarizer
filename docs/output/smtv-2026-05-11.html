Chapter 3 · A Bear Case

The Pattern Guy's Math Says the Nasdaq Has Hit Resistance

Bartaloney's measured moves stack across S&P, semis, and the Nasdaq itself, and the rest of the tape quietly refuses to confirm the new highs.

The reason this episode is worth a chapter is the guest. James Bartaloney has been reading charts professionally for 25 years and is the technician JC has called one of his first mentors in technical analysis. The week opened with Bart emailing the show that he wanted to short the Nasdaq. JC put him on. [31:00]

The framework is harmonic measured moves. Take the rally that ran into a correction, measure its length, project that same distance forward from the next low, and the index tends to bang into resistance at the projection. Bart's S&P chart goes back to the 2007 low. Each leg has hit the measured move and stalled. The current leg is now banging into the next one. [36:00]

The semis chart is the cleanest piece of the case. The semiconductor index ran straight into 1.27 times the original measured move, which is the square root of 1.618 (the golden ratio). It is the same kind of harmonic extension that has marked accelerations on this index for years. The Nasdaq itself is an ABCD pattern projected forward from the 1974 all-time low at 554. The composite is now sitting on the D leg. [37:20]

The confirmation is in the indices that did not make new highs on this rally. The NYSE Composite (the broadest US listed-equity index) failed to confirm. The Dow Jones Transports failed. The KBW Bank Index failed, and banks usually lead the tape both up and down. Bart's pet ratio, Staples versus the NYSE Composite (defensive stocks divided by broad equities, a quiet gauge of risk-off appetite), held its support level cleanly on the morning Wednesday gap up, which is a tell that defensive demand is still bid even on a euphoric session. [35:20]

Execution is the part the show hammered. Bart will not chase short into a parabolic move. He wants a daily signal reversal candle, an engulfing, an outside day, or a doji at the high, before stepping in. Without that print, the trade is wait. And critically, the call is a cool-off, with the Dow eventually exploding higher once the correction is in. Treat this as a tactical view at a stretched tape, the kind that respected pattern analysts publish at every major top whether one comes or not. [42:15]

The same framework flips bullish on long bonds. Bart has been long $TBT (the inverse 20-year Treasury ETF, a short-bond proxy) for years and is getting ready to close it and flip into long bonds in the coming weeks. The reasoning sits on the same harmonic math that points to one more leg lower in bond prices before a major reversal. $TBT off, $TLT on, possibly within a month. [44:40]
