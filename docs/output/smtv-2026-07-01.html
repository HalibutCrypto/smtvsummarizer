Bitcoin and Ether hit new lows, gold's ten-quarter run just snapped, and the companies that operate the US stock and futures markets keep collapsing while the market itself keeps printing highs.

Bitcoin and Ether spent the last day of Q2 on their lows and opened Q3 the same way. Both prints are back to levels not seen since late 2024, and total crypto market cap dropped through $2.01T, the softest number since last summer. On the desk, the language is bear-market language now. Not for the first time this year, and not with any conviction that this is the bottom.

Solana is the tell in the other direction. It sat at $74.50 +1.5% while everything else bled, and its ratio versus Ether just hit the highest level all year. Off its own local low a couple weeks ago, it's up about 25% while Bitcoin and Ether printed new ones. When the majors flatline or bleed and one alt runs, that alt is usually where the relative strength is telling you the next up leg starts.

Q2 closed with the Dow at record highs, its sixth consecutive quarterly gain. The NYSE Composite Index, a broad index that includes a lot of foreign listings on the New York exchange, ran the same six-for-six streak of record closes. Everything at all-time highs going into Independence Day week has been the base rate of this bull for months, and Q2 did not break the pattern.

Q3 opened red. SPX 7,530 -0.2%, NDX 30,280 -0.75%, Dow 52,500 -0.3%. Crude fell 1% to just under $69. Copper -1.7% to $6.15. VIX pushed 17 without really scaring anyone.

Gold sat flat around $4,040 and closed its first down quarter since 2023. Ten straight green quarterly candles, snapped. Silver was uglier, off 1.3% and back under $60. The bigger-picture uptrend is still intact, but the near-term setup on the desk is that silver retests the old $48-$50 zone that capped it for decades before the Hunt brothers rally in 1980. A pullback that runs longer than most gold bulls want.

Underneath the surface the rotation kept doing what it has done for weeks. Small-cap technology printed a god candle across the last two sessions. Small-cap healthcare and biotech are surging on breakouts big enough that the desk is treating them as initiation thrusts, the kind of move that shows up at the start of a new primary uptrend. Regional and community banks closed June at all-time highs. Home builders keep working. Coffee futures had their best session since 2021 yesterday. Every corner of the tape outside the Mag Seven is doing the work, and the desk is finally comfortable calling the Mag Seven the ATM for it.

The single-stock story of the morning was $META. It's launching a cloud storage business, and the whole cloud group is trading down on the news. $CRWV got hit on the open. It's the same playbook that took Snap out of Meta's copy of Stories, and every operator in that stack is going to have to price the risk that Zuck ships it for free.

The Companies That Run the Stock Market Are Down 30%. The Stock Market Is at All-Time Highs.

Tokenized equities are eating exchange revenue in real time, and the tape is starting to say it out loud.

Exchange stocks bleeding
  • $CBOE · down ~33% in six weeks
  • $CME · down ~33% in six weeks
  • $ICE · down ~33% on the year, massive top
  • $NDAQ · slightly less horrible, still ugly
The other side of the trade
  • $BK · Bank of NY Mellon, custodies the collateral
  • Tokenized $AAPL, $GOOGL live on chain
  • SpaceX had tokenized shares trading pre-IPO
  • 24/7 equity liquidity, cross-border access

The Dow just printed six record quarters in a row. Meanwhile $CME, $CBOE and $ICE, the three companies that operate the biggest US derivative and stock exchanges, are down roughly a third over the last six weeks. $NDAQ, the operator behind the Nasdaq index itself, is holding up a little better and still looks broken. The market their business exists to run is at record highs while the stocks themselves keep collapsing.

The one-word explanation on the desk is tokenization. SpaceX's IPO had tokenized shares trading before the actual IPO, which had never happened for any listing in history. Tokenized versions of $AAPL, $GOOGL and most of the S&P are now live on chain. For an investor in Kuala Lumpur or Lagos who cannot open a US brokerage account, that is the first working door to Wall Street. For a US investor, it is the first working door to stocks in countries that were never going to list here.

The desk had a live debate about how fast this goes. JC's guess for the day tokenized volume beats traditional exchange volume is April 20, 2031, roughly five years out. Strazza pushed back on the timeline, arguing that anyone who lived through 2008 remembers the Sunday nights when they wanted a regulated broker-dealer standing between them and their positions. Both of them were staring at the same chart of $CME rolling over.

The bank that keeps getting named on the winning side is $BK, Bank of New York Mellon. It is the plumbing bank most likely to hold the collateral that backs tokenized equities at institutional scale, and it keeps drifting higher while the exchange stocks give back 30% of their market cap. If you already live in crypto, none of this is news. Twenty-four seven markets have been the default for years. This is the equity world admitting it.
