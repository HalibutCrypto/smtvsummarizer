Bitcoin ticked up a percent to $62,900 and Ethereum went nowhere at $1,744. Solana did what it has done every session this week, which is hold its level while the rest of the majors decide what they want to do. That is now three straight days of quiet outperformance for $SOL against a flat-to-heavy tape, and the desk is still treating it as the only large-cap coin behaving like it works.

JC pulled up the BTC/gold ratio, a chart he has not looked at in a while. Against the dollar, Bitcoin looks like a stuck market. Against gold, which just added another percent to $4,130 while silver ripped +2.5% back above $60, it looks like a broken one. Same coin, two very different charts. If you were only allowed to watch one to size gross exposure, the gold-denominated version is the one screaming.

Jeff Degraf's $BTC to $30,000 call came up in passing. Strazza would not touch it. His logic was clean. If Bitcoin cuts in half again from here, the tape you would have to be looking at underneath is a stock market nowhere near 7,555 on the S&P. The two moves are not independent. So either the 30 handle is wrong, or the equity index level is wrong, and the desk is not sitting long semis into that second scenario.

Reader translation

“Speculators are the most bullish they’ve been in a decade” is CFTC positioning data. It reads how much of the futures market is leaning one way. Extreme long readings historically mark tops in the underlying because everyone who was going to buy already has. That is what Strazza was pointing at yesterday on the dollar, and it is the same lens he is applying to the “Bitcoin to 30” call today. Extreme predictions want extreme setups. This tape does not have one.

The tape opened green across the board and the Dow tagged another new all-time high at 52,675. NASDAQ 100 led up a full percent to 29,780, S&P added a third of a percent to 7,555, and thirty-year bond futures sat flat at 111. Crude was quiet at $74. Everything with any beta to a weaker dollar caught a bid. Copper up two percent to $6.25, gold up a percent to $4,130, silver ripping back above $60 for the first time in weeks.

The show's most durable read was underneath the index prints. Turning Point Research put up the 21-day rolling percentage of healthcare stocks outperforming the S&P, and the reading was the highest in fifteen years. Ninety percent of the sector outperforming the benchmark on a trailing-month basis is the kind of number that only shows up around the beginning of a regime shift. As recently as three weeks ago the XLV was the second-worst-performing sector of the year. It leads now, and the desk has been pounding the table on this since well before the breadth data confirmed the tape.

The most attributed name inside the sector is $UNH, where the desk is up something like 250% on December 2027 calls put on when the company's CEO was killed on the street in midtown Manhattan. The technical setup at the time was a 61.8% Fibonacci retracement from the 1987 crash low up to the recent highs, which the desk read as a floor. The medical-equipment sub-group is the catch-up trade being watched next, on the logic that if everything else in healthcare is working, devices should follow.

The crypto angle

A quiet, drifty index tape with metals ripping is a friendly backdrop for BTC in principle. Bitcoin is not participating today, and that is the point. When the whole world is chasing store-of-value assets and Bitcoin sits flat while gold and silver print new highs, the “digital gold” pitch is running behind physical for the second cycle in a row. That gap does not have to close inside the current move, but it is the read that keeps the desk cautious on $BTC even as they stay long metals and healthcare.

Chapter 3 · The Framework

Nvidia Slipped Under 20x and the Market Is Still Pricing Semis Like Cyclicals

The single framework worth writing down from today was Strazza's read on semiconductor multiples. Nvidia now trades below a 20x forward earnings multiple. That is under the S&P blended multiple of roughly 22-25. Micron, which the desk was calling “the best stock in the market” on air, trades at somewhere between a 6 and an 8 PE (forward earnings; for context, mature cyclicals like steel and oil trade there, mega-cap growth names live in the 30s and 40s).

The bull case in every strategist note is that semis are no longer cyclical, that AI infrastructure demand has re-rated the group into a secular growth trend the same way software got re-rated after 2013. If that were true, the multiples would show it. They do not. The market is stamping $NVDA and Micron with the exact same cyclical multiples they have always carried, and it is doing that in a year the entire narrative machine has been pointing the other direction.

Strazza's tape says two things are happening inside the group at once. The equipment names and the smaller semis are rolling over. Nvidia and Broadcom are re-accelerating out of a coiled bear flag. His book reflects it. He was short Intel puts, short Micron, short Qualcomm at the start of the week. By yesterday he had covered the shorts and bought five different Nvidia call contracts out to July 24, plus Broadcom, which was up four percent this morning after ripping yesterday. Being long and short the same sector in the same week is not a hedge. It is the bifurcation actually showing up in the book.

The uncomfortable version of the read, for anyone long the AI leaders because they believe the secular story, is that the multiples are the market's tell. If the crowd genuinely believed AI infrastructure demand had removed the boom-bust cycle from these companies forever, Micron would not sit at 8 times earnings. It sits there because the marginal buyer is still pricing in a down-cycle at some point. The stocks can still work from these levels. The framework the buyer is working from is not the one the strategists have been selling.
