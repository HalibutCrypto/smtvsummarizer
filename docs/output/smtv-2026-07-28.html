Chapter 3 · Framework

The Banks' Nineteen-Year Reclaim Looks a Lot Like Tech in 2019

The framework the show built up all morning was a chart comparison. The S&P bank index has just cleared its 2007 all-time highs, nineteen years after the great financial crisis. The reference cited on air was Louise Yamada, who has written for decades that the bigger the drawdown, the longer the time needed to repair. Nineteen years for banks fits the pattern exactly.

The setup the show wants the reader to sit with is the analog. Technology took roughly eighteen to nineteen years to fully clear its dot-com highs, and once it did in 2019 it went on to one of the largest secular runs in equity market history. Financials just did the same thing on the same clock. $XLF is already up eighty percent from the base breakout, similar to the run out of 2020 and the retest and rip out of the 2022 bear-market lows. The show's blunt question was whether financials have to run five times over the next seven years to complete the analog. A two-times run over the same window is far more modest and still fits the pattern.

Healthcare is the other quiet leader in the same mold. On a relative basis it is bouncing at exactly the same place it bounced twenty-five years ago. Johnson & Johnson, Merck, and Bristol Myers are printing new all-time highs or in the process of clearing multi-decade bases. UnitedHealth is up three hundred percent off its base. BridgeBio is consolidating above former highs. Biotech takeovers are running about four days a week. The reason it is getting no airtime is because there is no AI narrative you can draw a line to. The show's take is that if it walks like a duck and talks like a duck, it is probably not a chicken. Banks and biotech are both in year one of runs that historically go for years.
