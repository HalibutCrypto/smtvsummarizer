$BTC gave back roughly a thousand points into the open at $62,560. $ETH traded down -2.3% to $1,840. $SOL was the worst of the majors at $72.40 -1.5%, and total crypto market cap slipped to $2.13T.

The macro underneath these prices was actually friendly. The dollar index rolled back into an older range the desk has been watching. Japan and the US intervened in the yen over the weekend, which typically flushes dollar strength out of everything at once. Developed markets, China, and short-dollar assets from Poland to Mexico all broke out on their monthly candles. Gold and silver sat flat at $4,100 and $57.70. That is the exact macro setup Bitcoin usually loves, and Bitcoin was red anyway.

CZ tweeted over the weekend that crypto might be entering a bear market. JC's response on air was that crypto is a two-trillion-dollar rounding error in the middle of a raging bull market for stocks and does not get its own asset-class bear cycle. The argument runs either way, but the read is the same. When the tape is friendly and Bitcoin is red, the story is a crypto-specific one rather than a macro-driven one, and the show did not spend much time trying to guess which piece was calling the shot.

Chapter 2 · Lead Story

A Record Dow Monthly Close With the World Breaking Out Around It

The Dow closed July at 53,225, up +1.12% today and the highest monthly close in the index's history. The S&P added forty-one handles to 7,560 and the NASDAQ barely budged at 28,450, held back by the semiconductors that have been leaking for a month. The New York Stock Exchange composite, which weights the entire floor rather than one tech-heavy slice, also closed at all-time highs. On any traditional definition of a bull market, the tape is one.

The bigger monthly-chart story ran outside the S&P. Developed-market equities broke out cleanly. Japan, Europe, and Australia all posted charts the desk called some of their best in years. Emerging markets looked the same, and China ripped in July on huge monthly candles after $FXI (the flagship China ETF, dominated by Alibaba, Tencent-adjacent H-shares, and mainland financials) bottomed in the first days of the month and went in a straight line higher. Some of that flow rotated out of South Korea, Taiwan, and Japan into mainland names.

Energy told a similar story on a longer time frame. Crude got smoked another -6.5% to $79 on the Monday-morning routine of weekend headlines, but the majors zoomed out look better every week. $CVX, $XOM, and the refiners have already broken out. The desk was buying tanker names and long-dated calls on $PBR (Petrobras, the Brazilian state oil major).

The trade that made all of it hang together was the yen. Japan spent an estimated $36B over the weekend defending its currency in a coordinated buy with the US Treasury, and speculators who were net-short yen at multi-year extremes got run over. That reversal is dragging the dollar down, and everything that trades inversely to a stronger dollar is starting to work at once. JC said flatly that he likes short-dollar trades right now.

Bristol Myers and AstraZeneca surprised the market with merger talk overnight. Astra shareholders do not like it, Bristol shareholders do, and the tape ignored the whole story because nothing about a healthcare deal changes what monthly charts on eighteen indices are saying.

The crypto angle

Historically Bitcoin is the loudest short-dollar asset in the room. Every other short-dollar trade turned green this morning while $BTC traded red. Either the crypto tape catches up in the next few sessions as the broader dollar reversal grinds through, or the disconnect is a signal that positioning inside crypto is heavier than the macro read suggests. Watch that gap close, or refuse to close, before adding size.

Chapter 3 · Framework

Software Finally Gets Paid While the Semis Sit in the Rubble

Inside the S&P the split between software and semiconductors has become the loudest signal on the tape. Semis put together the greatest two-month run in the history of the sector across April and May, up roughly forty-two percent. June went flat. July closed down almost eight percent after touching down eleven percent intramonth. Small-cap tech, which had ripped in June, gave back almost fourteen percent last month.

Software went the other way. $MSFT was up another +4% this morning after last week's earnings and has now been rewarded for reporting good numbers three cycles in a row. It is the largest common-stock position on the desk. $NOW (ServiceNow, the enterprise workflow platform that sets the tone for software earnings season) has drifted higher for weeks after its own report. $PLTR (Palantir, the government-and-enterprise AI data platform) prints tonight into a chart the desk called a big polarity zone, and the tape wants a good print to fire the whole group. Even $ZM is trading like a software-plus-AI proxy because of its equity stake in Anthropic that could be worth up to ten billion dollars in a company with a twenty-seven-billion-dollar market cap, which is the same setup shape that put a rocket under $MSTR.

The framework worth writing down came from the panel discussion around Leopold, the trader who blew up a nine-figure book last week trading options on 2x single-stock ETFs. Skip the leverage angle. The interesting piece is what happens after a parabolic move exhausts itself. Momentum stocks do not gently step back into the normal four-stage life cycle that technicians teach. They distort it. Once the top prints, they often drift sideways in what Strazza called zombie mode for years before they come back. $PLUG (Plug Power, a fuel-cell name that briefly ran during the dot-com era) went nowhere for roughly two decades before running again. $OKLO (the small modular reactor stock that was a momentum favorite earlier this year) is doing the same thing right now, drifting sideways after a big blow-off.

Practically that means the read is to treat $SMH (the VanEck Semiconductor ETF, dominated by $NVDA, $TSM, and $AVGO) as topped until it proves otherwise. Software is the group that is actually working. Being aggressive there is more useful right now than trying to bottom-tick chips.
