The Dow Prints a Record on the Day Sam Altman Spooks the AI Trade

Broadcom and CrowdStrike got spanked despite blowout numbers, semis sold all morning, and the index split was the widest the desk had seen in months.

70 min · Watch the source on YouTube
TL;DR 5 min read

An offhand Sam Altman comment about AI spending got loose overnight, and the semis took it personally. Broadcom and CrowdStrike, two of the year's best stocks, both got smoked into their morning reactions. The Dow shrugged it off and printed a fresh all-time high anyway. The split between the headline indexes was the widest the desk had seen in months. The bigger argument on air was whether the two-month thrust in tech is the start of a new bull cycle or the end of this one.

  • Index dispersion the desk hadn't seen in months. Dow up roughly +1% at 51,250 (fresh all-time high), $SPX -0.33% at 7,544, NASDAQ -1.2% at 30,260.
  • $AVGO (Broadcom, the dominant custom-AI-silicon supplier behind hyperscaler ASICs) down roughly -9% to -15% after the print despite a blowout. $CRWD (CrowdStrike, endpoint security) down a similar amount. Both stocks were up +35% and +57% year-to-date going in.
  • The reason the desk circulated for the sell-off: Sam Altman publicly conceded that the hyperscalers cannot actually afford to keep buying AI compute at the current pace. The Uber CEO had already said the company blew through its full-year AI budget in the first quarter.
  • JC's central framework. The two-month rate of change in S&P technology is around +44%. The last time the desk saw a thrust like that was the 2002 dot-com bottom. Tech relative to the S&P just broke above its 2000 dot-com bubble highs after twenty-five years.
  • Crypto sold off overnight with the NASDAQ and bounced with it. $BTC hit $61,300 on the lows and was back to $63,400 by show time. $ETH $1,778 (low $1,718), $SOL $70 (-2.3%, low $67.50), total crypto cap roughly $2.19T.
  • Healthcare just printed thirty-year lows as a percentage of the S&P 500, with insider buying at fresh highs. JC's read is that large-cap healthcare ($AMGN, $GILD, $LLY, $UNH) is the contra-position to the tech-heavy index.
  • Volatility compression and a deteriorating breath regime are the conditions the desk keeps flagging. $VIX at 16, advance-decline lines peaked several months ago, only half the S&P is above its 50-day. The setup is the kindling for a vol spike. Whether one fires today is a separate question.

Crypto did what tech did overnight, only earlier and harder. $BTC sliced down to $61,300 on the worst of it and was back to roughly $63,400 by the time the show went live, a bounce of about $2,100 off the low. $ETH traded down to $1,718 and rebuilt to $1,778, which the desk noted in passing is the year of American independence and the asset's level on its country's two hundred fiftieth birthday. $SOL sat at $70 after hitting $67.50. Total crypto market cap printed roughly $2.19T after a low near $2.12T.

The pattern that matters is the correlation. When the AI-capex story hit the wires, Bitcoin traded as the highest-beta tech proxy in the building. Gold and silver caught the safety bid (both up +1.4%) and the thirty-year bond rallied. None of that flowed to crypto. The desk's working frame for owning Bitcoin here is the regret-minimization position: a small permanent allocation the holder agrees in advance not to look at, held for the same reason somebody owns Apple or any other dominant megacap. The next month is not the argument. The asset's tail outcomes are.

The opening read of the morning was the rare green-on-red split across the three big indexes. The Dow up almost a full percent into fresh all-time highs while the NASDAQ shed 360 points. Spencer flagged it as the kind of dispersion he could not remember seeing recently, and the rest of the show traced where the weakness was coming from.

The proximate trigger was an earnings reaction the desk called ugly. $AVGO (Broadcom, the company that designs the custom AI chips behind a lot of hyperscaler infrastructure) printed and the stock opened down somewhere between nine and fifteen percent depending on the hour. $CRWD (CrowdStrike, the endpoint-security name that has been one of the cleanest software trades of the year) did similar damage. The detail the desk circled was that Broadcom's CFO appeared to read off the wrong quarter's numbers on the conference call before restarting. JC's note was that the slip is not the reason for the sell-off, but the slip is consistent with the kind of unforced error a tape that has stopped giving slack tends to punish. Both names were up +35% and +57% year-to-date going into the print, so part of the reaction is a stretched rubber band reverting on its own.

The narrative the desk credited for the wider weakness landed two days ago at a conference. Sam Altman flippantly conceded that the cost of AI compute has grown to a point the customer base cannot actually absorb at this run-rate, and that the CEOs of the companies buying it have started saying so out loud. The Uber CEO had already told a podcast that the company burned its full-year AI budget in the first quarter. JC's frame is that the build-out is the cleanest version of an arms race the market has ever priced, and that the first sign of capex fatigue at the customer level was always going to bite the names that supply the customer. Broadcom and CrowdStrike are on that list. The Alphabet eighty-billion secondary the desk dissected on Monday is the same story from the supplier side. Big tech taps the equity market for that kind of size when the credit market stops absorbing the round, and the credit market stops absorbing the round when the lender starts pricing the same fatigue.

Chapter 3 · Framework

The Two-Month Tech Thrust Hasn't Shown Up Since the 2002 Bottom

The chart JC ran for most of the segment was the monthly candlestick of the S&P 500 technology sector. The two most recent monthly bars are, in his word, monsters. The two-month rate of change is roughly +44%. The desk pulled the history. The last time a tech momentum thrust of that size printed on the monthly was 2002, off the bottom of the dot-com crash. Every other instance of a thrust like that, across small caps, China, South Korea, and the regimes the desk has tracked for a decade, has shown up early in a new bull cycle. None of them have shown up at the end of one.

What makes the 2026 print weird is the position of the thrust. Every prior instance lived at the start of a new regime, after a wash-out. This one is happening at all-time highs in the middle of a raging bull market. JC's argument is that the data still reads as initiation, and that selling tech here on the rate-of-change alone has no historical precedent. The counter on the desk is the volatility-compression and breath-deterioration setup. Advance-decline lines peaked several months ago. Only half the S&P is trading above its 50-day moving average. The $VIX has compressed even with equities making new highs because the same call-buying pile that has driven the upside is also pushing measured volatility (which weighs both calls and puts) higher. The condition the desk reads here is the beach ball held under water, where compression eventually gives way to a spike when the first real headline lands. The condition can persist for weeks. The condition has been persisting for weeks.

A companion chart pushed the argument further. Tech relative to the S&P 500, the ratio chart, just broke above its 2000 dot-com bubble peaks. Twenty-five years to retake that line. Nobody on the desk thinks of tech as having anywhere to go from here precisely because tech has been the dominant trade since 2010. The argument is that the 2000 line is a structural one, and a structural breakout above a structural line is not how secular tops historically read.

The flip side of the index

JC's contra-position to the tech-heavy print is large-cap healthcare. Healthcare's weight in the S&P 500 just printed thirty-year lows. Consumer staples sit at 4.5% of the index, utilities at 2%, both at all-time lows in the sector representation chart the desk pulled from Ned Davis. Tech is pushing 40%. Corporate-insider buying in healthcare just printed fresh highs per the Sentiment Trader composite. JC flagged $AMGN pulling back to a former resistance, $GILD at a logical level, $LLY through a trillion and the cleanest chart in the group, and $UNH finishing a textbook five-wave decline after the year-ago CEO killing on a Manhattan sidewalk. Nobody wants any of it, and the people running the companies are quietly writing checks for their own stock.

The fixed-income angle the desk kept circling sits underneath the equity debate. Volatility in the ten-year has compressed alongside everything else, the COT positioning in bond futures looks unusual, and the new Fed chair the market is calibrating to (a Trump pick the desk took a few Home Alone jokes about by name) has signaled he intends to be more independent than the White House wanted. The desk read fixed income as the next place vol expansion shows up, and the carry-on read is that a bond-market storm passes through equities first.

The Bottom Line

  1. The headline indexes split the hardest they have in months and the cause was the AI-capex story finally biting. Dow up +1% at a fresh record, NASDAQ -1.2%, $AVGO and $CRWD both spanked despite blowout numbers. Sam Altman conceded out loud that the customers cannot afford the current AI run-rate. The Alphabet secondary earlier in the week was the supplier-side version of the same fatigue.

A +44% rate of change has not shown up since the 2002 dot-com bottom, and tech relative to the S&P just retook the 2000 highs after twenty-five years. The contra-data is real: breath has been deteriorating for months and the $VIX at 16 sits in the compressed state that historically precedes a spike. Both reads can be true and the framework gets tested by which one resolves first.
  3. The crypto behavior today was the high-beta tech proxy trade, and the contrarian set-up the desk likes is large-cap healthcare. $BTC bounced from $61,300 to $63,400 in lockstep with the NASDAQ and ignored the metals bid.
