The Dow Jones Industrial Average closed yesterday at 51,999.67, the highest close in recorded history, and futures sit flat at 52,420 ahead of the Fed. S&P 500 futures are unchanged at 7,590, NASDAQ 100 is leading at +0.5% to 30,480, and the VIX is back near 16. Bonds are quietly bid, with the 30-year future flat at 113 and the desk noting that the long bond has been the asset class with the best chart for weeks. Crude added +1% to $76, still landing in the middle of the post-conflict range that traded as low as $67 pre-invasion and spiked to $119 at the February peak. Gold sits flat at $4,350, silver flat near $70.[8:29]

The actual story sits underneath the print. JC walked through the run of capital raises the market has cleared in the last few months and made the spreadsheet do the talking. $NVDA went out for $20 billion of fresh debt and the order book filled at $85 billion. The company shrugged, took $25 billion, and walked away. $GOOGL raised $80 billion with Berkshire Hathaway underwriting $10 billion of it. OpenAI raised $122 billion a couple of months ago. Anthropic raised another monster round. SpaceX printed the biggest IPO in market history last Friday and is still grinding higher with options live. There is a hum on the tape that says when companies ask for capital, investors are showing up with checks, and the checks are larger than what was asked for.[3:01]

The Fed sits inside this backdrop as scenery. Today is the first FOMC under Kevin Warsh, the new chair. Spencer asked three times in the open whether anyone on the desk actually cares about the meeting. JC's answer was that he was surprised the Fed still meets eight times a year for what amounts to ceremony, and that the bond market has already decided where it wants to go. Strazza was excited by exactly one thing, which is that Warsh is reported to want less Fed speak. Less talking, less disclosures, fewer parade-of-committee-members at every economic club around the country. After two terms of running the country on a Fed addiction, that dial-back is the lone signal worth pricing in.[11:04]

Robotics, the short version

JC keeps getting asked about robotics and the answer is that the headline ETFs are built badly because the indices behind them lag the theme. The five names that are actually working break into three buckets. The body of the robot is $ROK (Rockwell Automation) and $TER (Teradyne), both industrial workhorses. The nervous system is $TXN (Texas Instruments) and $MCHP (Microchip), the analog and microcontroller layer. The brain is $NXPI (NXP Semiconductors), which sits closest to the autonomy stack. Strazza's biggest position is $ABBNY, the over-the-counter line of Swiss industrial ABB, which is as close to a pure-play robotics megacap as the market offers.[18:18]

Chapter 3 · The Sentiment Paradox

Phil Pearlman Asked Why Everyone Is Trying to Short the Highs. JC Said That Is the Bull Case.

Phil Pearlman came on late in the show and asked the question the desk had been dancing around all morning. With the animal spirits this loud and the Dow at fresh records, why are there still so many people on Twitter and StockTwits begging him for the two-times inverse SpaceX ETF, trying to short the strongest stocks in the strongest tape of their lifetime, going against the grain and getting destroyed every session. He framed it in psychological terms, reaching for the Freud word for the death drive. The version a market reader cares about is the same one. Something in a large slice of the audience wants to take the steering wheel and aim for the bridge.[61:37]

JC's answer was the most useful framework of the show. He started by refusing the why. Why people feel what they feel is not a tractable question. The data is. Last week the American Association of Individual Investors survey, which polls retail investors on the next six months, showed the fewest bulls all year and roughly half the sample bearish. That print landed on the same day the Dow closed at a fresh all-time high. The S&P bank index is breaking above its 2007 great financial crisis highs as the desk speaks, which is the same kind of multi-decade base resolution that powered the run after 1929 took 25 years to round-trip. Consumer sentiment at the lowest levels ever with the stock market at the highest levels ever is a great combination for people who own stocks. The gap is not the warning sign. The gap is the fuel.[64:54]

From there JC stacked the capital deluge against the survey. Companies asked for money and the market wrote checks every time. There is one observation he flagged as a near-certainty for some future date that is not this date. Human beings are incapable of discovering a genuinely transformative technology without eventually getting carried away. The day the capital stops showing up will be the day to worry. Today the capital is showing up in volumes that round to surprise. His practical advice for anyone caught between Twitter rage and the print was direct. Turn the financial television off. The angry guy on Twitter shorting SpaceX is mostly sad he is not making money in a bull market because he does not have any money to put in. None of that is your problem as the holder of assets. Focus on price.[66:58]

The crypto angle

The same shape applies on the digital side. $BTC spent today red while every macro variable that usually correlates with a BTC bid was lined up bullishly. Strazza's line was that people still hate crypto and cannot enjoy a single decent update without selling it back. That is the exact crypto version of the AAII print. When the sentiment audience hates the asset and the chart still holds the trend, that is the same setup the desk is describing for equities. The catalyst lands when the people who hate it finally give up and pay up.
